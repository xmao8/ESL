Riding the Bus: Asking the Driver for Help

Characters:

  • DRIVER: A city bus driver, experienced and helpful.
  • TARIQ: A job seeker riding the bus for the first time.

Scene: Tariq needs to take the bus to a job interview, but he’s never used public transit here. He asks the driver for help as he boards.

Conversation

TARIQ: Good morning! Does this bus go to the shopping center on Lake Avenue?

DRIVER: Morning! Yes, the Number 42 goes right past it. Hop on!

TARIQ: Great. How much is the fare?

DRIVER: $2.00 exactly. Do you have exact change? The machine doesn’t give change.

TARIQ: I have two dollar bills. Where do I put them?

DRIVER: Just feed them into the slot right here. Perfect. Now grab a seat — we’re pulling out.

TARIQ: Thank you. How will I know when to get off?

DRIVER: I’ll announce the major stops. The shopping center is about 15 minutes away — it’s the stop after the high school.

TARIQ: Could you let me know when we get there? I don’t want to miss my stop.

DRIVER: Sure thing. I’ll give you a shout. Is this your first time on this route?

TARIQ: First time on any bus here, actually. I have a job interview at 10:00. Will I make it on time?

DRIVER: It’s 9:20 now, so you’ll arrive around 9:40. Plenty of time. Good luck with the interview!

TARIQ: Thanks! Do I need to pull a cord or press a button to request the stop?

DRIVER: Press the yellow strip on the window or pull the cord — either one signals me to stop. Do it about a block before.

TARIQ: Got it. One more question — how do I get back? Is it the same bus?

DRIVER: Same number, opposite direction. Cross the street and wait at the shelter with the Number 42 sign.

TARIQ: And if I ride the bus every day for work, is there a cheaper pass?

DRIVER: Definitely. A monthly pass is $65 — it pays for itself if you ride more than twice a day. You can buy one at the transit center downtown.

TARIQ: That’s good to know. I really appreciate all your help.

DRIVER: Anytime! And here’s your stop coming up — shopping center, next!

TARIQ: Thank you! Have a great day!

DRIVER: You too — knock that interview out of the park!

— THE END —

Vocabulary List

  • Fare /fer/ (n.) the price of a bus or taxi ride. Example: How much is the fare?
  • Exact change /ɪɡˈzækt tʃeɪndʒ/ (n.) the precise amount of money. Example: Do you have exact change?
  • Hop on /hɑːp ɑːn/ (phr v.) to get on a bus (informal). Example: Hop on!
  • Pull out /pʊl aʊt/ (phr v.) to start moving (for a vehicle). Example: We’re pulling out.
  • Announce /əˈnaʊns/ (v.) to say loudly for people to hear. Example: I’ll announce the major stops.
  • Miss my stop /mɪs maɪ stɑːp/ (phr.) to not get off where planned. Example: I don’t want to miss my stop.
  • Request a stop /rɪˈkwest ə stɑːp/ (phr.) to signal you want to get off. Example: Pull the cord to request the stop.
  • Shelter /ˈʃeltər/ (n.) a covered bus stop. Example: Wait at the shelter across the street.
  • Monthly pass /ˈmʌnθli pæs/ (n.) a ticket for unlimited rides for a month. Example: A monthly pass is $65.
  • Pays for itself /peɪz fɔːr ɪtˈself/ (phr.) saves enough to cover its cost. Example: It pays for itself quickly.
