Negotiating a Medical Bill

Characters:

  • KAREN: A billing specialist at a hospital, professional and helpful.
  • OLGA: A house cleaner facing a large medical bill.

Scene: Olga received a $2,400 hospital bill she can’t afford. She’s calling the billing department to ask about her options. Karen answers.

Conversation

KAREN: Hello, billing department. This is Karen. How may I help you?

OLGA: Hi, Karen. My name is Olga Petrova. I received a bill for $2,400 from my emergency room visit, and I can’t afford to pay it all at once.

KAREN: I understand, Ms. Petrova. Many patients feel the same way. Let me look up your account.

OLGA: Thank you. The account number is 4-0-8-1-5-2.

KAREN: I found it. The total is $2,400 for the ER visit on September 3rd. First, let me ask — do you have health insurance?

OLGA: No, I don’t. I’m self-employed, and insurance is too expensive for me.

KAREN: Okay. Did you know we offer a self-pay discount? Uninsured patients automatically get 30% off.

OLGA: Really? Nobody told me that. So the bill isn’t actually $2,400?

KAREN: Correct. With the discount, your balance is $1,680. I’ve just applied it to your account.

OLGA: That’s much better, but $1,680 is still a lot for me. Can I pay in installments?

KAREN: Absolutely. We offer interest-free payment plans. How much could you comfortably pay each month?

OLGA: Maybe $100 a month? Would that be acceptable?

KAREN: That works. At $100 a month, you’d be paid off in about 17 months, with zero interest.

OLGA: Zero interest — that’s wonderful. Is there any fee to set up the plan?

KAREN: No fees at all. I can set it up right now over the phone.

OLGA: Please do. One question — will this affect my credit score?

KAREN: As long as you make your payments on time, we won’t report anything negative. The key is to stay current.

OLGA: I will. Can I pay by automatic withdrawal from my bank account?

KAREN: Yes, and I recommend it — you’ll never miss a payment. I just need your routing and account numbers.

OLGA: Let me get my checkbook… Okay, I’m ready.

Olga reads the numbers, and Karen sets up the payment plan.

KAREN: You’re all set, Ms. Petrova. Your first payment of $100 will come out on the 15th.

OLGA: Thank you, Karen. I was so stressed about this bill, and now I feel like I can breathe again.

KAREN: I’m glad I could help. Remember — always call us before a bill becomes a crisis.

— THE END —

Vocabulary List

  • Negotiate /nɪˈɡoʊʃieɪt/ (v.) to discuss to reach an agreement. Example: She’s negotiating her medical bill.
  • Billing department /ˈbɪlɪŋ dɪˈpɑːrtmənt/ (n.) the office handling payments. Example: Hello, billing department.
  • Self-employed /ˌself ɪmˈplɔɪd/ (adj.) working for yourself. Example: I’m self-employed.
  • Self-pay discount /self peɪ ˈdɪskaʊnt/ (n.) a lower price for uninsured patients. Example: We offer a self-pay discount.
  • Installment /ɪnˈstɔːlmənt/ (n.) one of several partial payments. Example: Can I pay in installments?
  • Interest-free /ˈɪntrəst friː/ (adj.) with no extra charge for borrowing. Example: Interest-free payment plans.
  • Paid off /peɪd ɔːf/ (phr.) fully paid. Example: You’d be paid off in 17 months.
  • Credit score /ˈkredɪt skɔːr/ (n.) a number showing how reliable you pay. Example: Will this affect my credit score?
  • Automatic withdrawal /ˌɔːtəˈmætɪk wɪðˈdrɔːəl/ (n.) money taken automatically. Example: Pay by automatic withdrawal.
  • Stay current /steɪ ˈkɜːrənt/ (phr.) to keep payments up to date. Example: The key is to stay current.
