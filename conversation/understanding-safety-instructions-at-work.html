Understanding Safety Instructions at Work

Characters:

  • ROBERT: A safety trainer at a warehouse, serious but friendly.
  • YUKI: A new warehouse employee on her first day.

Scene: It’s Yuki’s first day at a warehouse. Robert, the safety trainer, is explaining the safety rules before Yuki starts work.

Conversation

ROBERT: Welcome aboard, Yuki! Before you start, we need to go over the safety rules. This is the most important part of your training.

YUKI: I’m ready. Safety first, right?

ROBERT: Exactly. Rule number one: always wear your safety vest, hard hat, and steel-toe boots in the warehouse. No exceptions.

YUKI: I have the vest and boots. Where do I get a hard hat?

ROBERT: I’ll give you one after this session. Rule number two: never walk under a raised forklift load.

YUKI: What does “raised load” mean?

ROBERT: Good question. When a forklift lifts a pallet high into the air, never walk underneath it. If the pallet falls, it could seriously injure you.

YUKI: Understood. I’ll stay clear of forklifts.

ROBERT: Also, make eye contact with the forklift driver before crossing in front of one. They need to see you.

YUKI: That makes sense. What should I do if I see a spill on the floor?

ROBERT: Don’t try to clean chemicals yourself. Put up a caution sign and report it to your supervisor immediately.

YUKI: What about water spills? Can I mop those?

ROBERT: Yes, water is fine to mop, but still put up the sign first so nobody slips.

YUKI: Where are the fire extinguishers and emergency exits?

ROBERT: Great question. There’s an extinguisher every 50 feet along the walls — see the red cabinets? And the emergency exits are marked with green signs.

YUKI: I see them. What’s the emergency number here?

ROBERT: Dial 911 for real emergencies. For smaller issues, call extension 4000 — that’s the safety office.

YUKI: If I don’t understand an instruction, is it okay to ask? I don’t want to slow anyone down.

ROBERT: Always ask! There are no stupid questions when it comes to safety. I’d rather explain something twice than see someone get hurt.

YUKI: Thank you, Robert. I feel much more confident now.

ROBERT: You’re going to do great, Yuki. Now let’s get your hard hat and take a tour of the floor.

— THE END —

Vocabulary List

  • Safety vest /ˈseɪfti vest/ (n.) a bright vest that makes workers visible. Example: Always wear your safety vest.
  • Hard hat /hɑːrd hæt/ (n.) a strong helmet protecting the head. Example: Wear your hard hat in the warehouse.
  • Steel-toe boots /stiːl toʊ buːts/ (n.) boots with metal protecting the toes. Example: Steel-toe boots are required.
  • No exceptions /noʊ ɪkˈsepʃənz/ (phr.) the rule applies to everyone. Example: No exceptions.
  • Forklift /ˈfɔːrklɪft/ (n.) a vehicle that lifts heavy pallets. Example: Never walk under a raised forklift load.
  • Pallet /ˈpælət/ (n.) a flat platform for storing goods. Example: When a forklift lifts a pallet…
  • Caution sign /ˈkɔːʃən saɪn/ (n.) a sign warning of danger. Example: Put up a caution sign.
  • Fire extinguisher /ˈfaɪər ɪkˈstɪŋɡwɪʃər/ (n.) a device that puts out small fires. Example: Extinguishers are every 50 feet.
  • Emergency exit /iːˈmɜːrdʒənsi ˈeɡzɪt/ (n.) a door for leaving quickly in danger. Example: Exits are marked with green signs.
  • Extension /ɪkˈstenʃən/ (n.) a short internal phone number. Example: Call extension 4000.
