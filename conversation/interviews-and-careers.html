A Job Interview

Setting: A kitchen table in the evening. Maya has a job interview tomorrow morning for an office job, and her friend Carlos — a general manager at a store — is helping her prepare. They talk about what to wear, what to say, and how to sound confident. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Maya — has a job interview tomorrow for an office job; nervous but motivated.
  • Carlos — Maya's friend, a general manager at a store; has done many job interviews.

Carlos: Maya! You look nervous. Is everything okay?

Maya: I have a job interview tomorrow morning, and I am preparing my answers.

Carlos: That is exciting! What job is it?

Maya: It is an office job. I would rather work in an office than outdoors, because I like a quiet place.

Carlos: Good. And what will you wear?

Maya: I would rather wear a long-sleeved shirt than a t-shirt. A t-shirt is too casual for an interview.

Carlos: Smart. The interviewer will notice that. What questions do you think they will ask?

Maya: Probably, “Tell me about yourself.” I want to sound confident and enthusiastic.

Carlos: You are confident! And you are honest and helpful — those are good qualities to talk about.

Maya: Thank you. I also want to talk about my communication skills. I speak clearly, and I listen well.

Carlos: That is important. A general manager needs communication skills and leadership skills.

Maya: I know. I would rather work on a team than work alone, too. I like helping people.

Carlos: Then say that! Enthusiasm matters. Show them you really want the job.

Maya: I will. But what if they ask about money? The average annual salary is in the job ad, but I am not sure what to say.

Carlos: Just be honest. You can say, “I would rather talk about the job first, and we can discuss salary later.”

Maya: That is a good answer. Polite and honest at the same time.

Carlos: Exactly. And remember — arrive early. I would rather you arrived ten minutes early than two minutes late.

Maya: I will arrive early. I would rather not be late for my first interview!

Carlos: One more tip: smile, and speak slowly. Self-confidence grows when you breathe and take your time.

Maya: Thank you, Carlos. You are a thoughtful friend.

Carlos: You will do well. Would you rather practice with me now, or rest tonight?

Maya: I would rather practice now — just a few questions. Then I can sleep well.

Carlos: Okay. Question one: why do you want this job?

Maya: Because I am motivated, I like helping people, and I would rather do work I love.

Carlos: Perfect answer. Now, question two …

🎯 Speaking Challenge

  • Use 3 new vocabulary words in your conversation.
  • Talk about YOUR life, not just the characters'.
  • After you finish: your teacher will share useful corrections they heard — during the conversation, focus on speaking, not on being perfect.

Vocabulary List

  • Confident /ˈkɑnfɪdənt/ (adj.) sure of your own skills. Example: A confident person speaks clearly in an interview.
  • Enthusiastic /ɪnˌθuziˈæstɪk/ (adj.) full of energy and interest. Example: An enthusiastic worker smiles and asks good questions.
  • Honest /ˈɑnɪst/ (adj.) always telling the truth. Example: Be honest about your skills and your experience.
  • Thoughtful /ˈθɔtfəl/ (adj.) thinking about other people's feelings. Example: A thoughtful manager listens to the team.
  • Arrogant /ˈærəɡənt/ (adj.) thinking you are better than everyone else. Example: Do not sound arrogant in an interview.
  • Sneaky /ˈsniki/ (adj.) doing things in secret, in a dishonest way. Example: A sneaky worker hides mistakes.
  • Self-confidence /ˌsɛlfˈkɑnfɪdəns/ (n.) belief in your own skills. Example: Good self-confidence helps you speak clearly.
  • Enthusiasm /ɪnˈθuziˌæzəm/ (n.) strong interest and energy. Example: Show enthusiasm when you talk about the job.
  • Long-sleeved shirt /ˈlɔŋ ˈslivd ˈʃɜrt/ (n.) a shirt with sleeves that cover your arms. Example: Wear a long-sleeved shirt to the interview.
  • T-shirt /ˈti ˈʃɜrt/ (n.) a casual shirt with short sleeves. Example: Do not wear a t-shirt to a job interview.
  • General manager /ˈdʒɛnərəl ˈmænɪdʒər/ (n.) the person who leads a whole store or company. Example: The general manager hires new workers.
  • Communication skills /kəˌmjunəˈkeɪʃən ˈskɪlz/ (n.) the ability to speak clearly and listen well. Example: Good communication skills help a team work well.
