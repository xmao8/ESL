Asking for Information at the Community Center

Setting: Saturday morning at the community center. Rosa just moved to town and visits the information desk, where Sam, a volunteer, helps newcomers. Rosa asks information questions to plan her errands. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Rosa — new in town; she has a long list of errands and lots of questions.
  • Sam — a friendly community center volunteer who knows the town well.

Rosa: Excuse me — is this the information desk?

Sam: It is! I'm Sam, the volunteer. What can I help you with?

Rosa: I just moved here. I need to do some things in town, but I don't know where to go.

Sam: That's exactly why we're here. What's first on your list?

Rosa: Where can I mail packages?

Sam: At the post office on Center Street — it's right next to the library.

Rosa: What time does the library open on Sundays?

Sam: At one o'clock in the afternoon. And the post office opens at nine on Saturdays.

Rosa: Good. How much does it cost to mail a package?

Sam: It depends on the size. A small box is about five dollars.

Rosa: I also want to open a checking account. Where is the nearest bank?

Sam: There's a bank across the street from the post office. Very convenient.

Rosa: What do I need to open a checking account?

Sam: Bring your passport and some money for the minimum deposit.

Rosa: What is the minimum deposit?

Sam: It's different at every bank — usually fifty or a hundred dollars.

Rosa: How do I pay at stores? Can I get a debit card?

Sam: Yes. Most banks give you a debit card with a new checking account.

Rosa: One more thing. I bought a used car — where do I go to register my car?

Sam: You register your car at the DMV on Hill Road, about three miles from here.

Rosa: How far is that by bus?

Sam: About twenty minutes. You can pick up bus schedules at the stop sign outside.

Rosa: How often does the bus come here?

Sam: Every fifteen minutes during the day, every thirty at night.

Rosa: Do I need a driver's license for that? I only have my state ID.

Sam: No — you don't need a driver's license to ride the bus. Your state ID is fine.

Rosa: Thank you so much! You answered all my questions, Sam.

Sam: Anytime! Welcome to our community, Rosa.

🎯 Speaking Challenge

  • Use 3 new vocabulary words in your conversation.
  • Ask information questions (Where, What time, How much, How often) about YOUR community.
  • Switch roles: one person is the volunteer, the other is new in town. Talk about YOUR life, not just the characters'.

Vocabulary List

  • Post office /poʊst ˈɔːfɪs/ (n.) the place where you send and receive mail. Example: Where can I mail packages? At the post office.
  • Mail packages /meɪl ˈpækɪdʒɪz/ (phr.) to send boxes or parcels by post. Example: I need to mail packages at the post office.
  • Checking account /ˈtʃekɪŋ əˈkaʊnt/ (n.) a bank account for everyday spending. Example: I want to open a checking account.
  • Minimum deposit /ˈmɪnɪməm dɪˈpɑːzɪt/ (n.) the smallest first amount of money you must put in a new account. Example: The minimum deposit is fifty dollars.
  • Debit card /ˈdebɪt kɑːrd/ (n.) a bank card that takes money directly from your account. Example: Pay with a debit card — you need no cash.
  • Library /ˈlaɪbreri/ (n.) a place with free books and computers. Example: The library opens at one on Sundays.
  • Bus schedules /bʌs ˈskedʒuːlz/ (phr.) printed times showing when buses come. Example: Pick up bus schedules at the bus stop.
  • DMV /ˌdiː em ˈviː/ (n.) the office for driver's licenses and car registration. Example: You register your car at the DMV.
  • Register a car /ˈredʒɪstər ə kɑːr/ (phr.) to officially record your car with the state. Example: I need to register my car this week.
  • State ID /steɪt aɪ diː/ (n.) an official ID card for people who do not drive. Example: I use my state ID at the bank.
