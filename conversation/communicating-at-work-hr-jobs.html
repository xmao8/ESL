At the HR Office: Talking About HR Jobs

Setting: Tuesday morning at the HR office of a big company. Ana is at a job interview for an HR Assistant job, and Mr. Chen, the HR Coordinator, asks her about her experience and answers her polite questions. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Ana — a job applicant; she worked at a small office for two years and wants to work as an HR Assistant.
  • Mr. Chen — the HR Coordinator at the company; he talks to job applicants, plans interviews, and explains HR jobs.

Mr. Chen: Good morning, Ana. Thank you for coming in today. Please, sit down.

Ana: Good morning, Mr. Chen. Thank you for inviting me. Would you mind telling me a little about this job first?

Mr. Chen: Of course not. We want to hire an HR Assistant for our team. Could you tell me about your experience?

Ana: Yes. I worked at a small office for two years. I answered phones and helped new workers.

Mr. Chen: Good. Our Recruiter talks to job applicants first, and then I meet the best ones. What are you good at?

Ana: I am good at talking to people and organizing information. Could you tell me who I would work with?

Mr. Chen: You would work with me and the HR Manager. The HR Manager makes the big decisions for our team.

Ana: I see. Would you mind explaining what the HR Coordinator does every day?

Mr. Chen: Sure, no problem. The HR Coordinator plans interviews and training for new workers.

Ana: That sounds interesting. Can you tell me about training? Would the company train me?

Mr. Chen: Yes. We train every new worker for two weeks. We help new workers — we never criticize them.

Ana: That is good to hear. Would you mind telling me about the pay? Who pays the workers here?

Mr. Chen: Our Payroll Specialist pays everyone on the last Friday of the month. And our Benefits Specialist helps with health insurance.

Ana: Could you tell me about vacation time? How many days do workers get?

Mr. Chen: Sure. New workers get ten days. Would you mind asking me about something else?

Ana: Yes — how does the company recruit new workers? Do you recruit at schools?

Mr. Chen: We recruit online and at job fairs. We almost never fire a worker — we train and help them first.

Ana: That is very polite. One more question: is the office formal or informal?

Mr. Chen: We are friendly but polite. We use formal words in emails to HR, and informal words with close coworkers. My boss gave our team a compliment last week — she said we are polite and helpful.

Ana: Thank you very much, Mr. Chen. I would love to work here. Could you tell me when you will decide?

Mr. Chen: We will call you this week. Thank you, Ana. It was nice to meet you.

🎯 Speaking Challenge

  • Use all 3 polite request forms: Would you mind…?, Could you…?, and Can you…?
  • Student A is the HR Coordinator, Student B is the job applicant. Ask 3 questions about the job — pay, hours, training, or vacation. Use at least 3 vocabulary words.
  • Switch roles and ask about a different job — Recruiter, Payroll Specialist, or Benefits Specialist. Talk about YOUR life, not just the characters'.

Vocabulary List

  • recruit /rɪˈkruːt/ (v.) to ask someone to join your company. Example: We recruit new workers every spring.
  • hire /ˈhaɪər/ (v.) to give someone a job. Example: The company hopes to hire an HR Assistant this month.
  • train /treɪn/ (v.) to teach a new worker how to do the job. Example: The HR Coordinator will train you on Monday.
  • fire /ˈfaɪər/ (v.) to tell a worker they must leave the job. Example: The company almost never fires a good worker.
  • HR Manager /ˌeɪtʃˈɑːr ˈmænɪdʒər/ (n.) the person who manages the HR team. Example: The HR Manager makes the big decisions.
  • HR Assistant /ˌeɪtʃˈɑːr əˈsɪstənt/ (n.) a person who helps with HR paperwork and questions. Example: Ana wants to work as an HR Assistant.
  • HR Coordinator /ˌeɪtʃˈɑːr koʊˈɔːrdɪneɪtər/ (n.) a person who plans interviews and training. Example: The HR Coordinator plans the interviews.
  • Recruiter /rɪˈkruːtər/ (n.) a person who finds new workers for a company. Example: The Recruiter talks to job applicants first.
  • Payroll Specialist /ˈpeɪroʊl ˈspeʃəlɪst/ (n.) a person who pays the workers. Example: The Payroll Specialist pays everyone on Friday.
  • Benefits Specialist /ˈbenəfɪts ˈspeʃəlɪst/ (n.) a person who helps workers with health insurance and vacation time. Example: Ask the Benefits Specialist about health insurance.
  • compliment /ˈkɑːmpləmənt/ (v.) to say something nice about someone's work. Example: My boss likes to compliment our team.
  • criticize /ˈkrɪtəsaɪz/ (v.) to say someone's work is not good. Example: A good boss does not criticize workers in front of others.
  • formal /ˈfɔːrməl/ (adj.) serious and polite, for official situations. Example: Use formal words in an email to HR.
  • informal /ɪnˈfɔːrməl/ (adj.) relaxed and friendly, not official. Example: We use informal words with close coworkers.
  • polite /pəˈlaɪt/ (adj.) using 'please' and 'thank you'; showing good manners. Example: It is polite to say 'Could you help me?'
