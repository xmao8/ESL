Weekend Plans

Setting: Friday afternoon at a café near their workplace. Lena, Carlos, and Maya are sitting around a table with coffee, making plans for the weekend. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Lena — tired after a long week; prefers quiet, relaxing weekends.
  • Carlos — energetic; loves sports, music, and doing things with friends.
  • Maya — friendly and organized; taking a Saturday pottery class.

Carlos: So, what’s everyone doing this weekend? I need some good ideas!

Maya: Hmm, I’m going to a pottery class on Saturday. It’s my third time. I love it!

Lena: That sounds nice, but messy. I think I’ll just stay home and read. I’ve had a long week.

Carlos: Read? Come on, Lena, it’s the weekend! Let’s do something exciting — hiking, maybe?

Lena: (Laughs) Hiking? That sounds like more work! I want to relax. My legs are still tired from last weekend’s shopping trip.

Maya: I agree with both of you. Pottery helps me relax and be active. You shape the clay, and it feels so calming.

Carlos: Sounds artsy! But I like moving. I was thinking of biking or going to the lake.

Lena: Maybe I’ll try pottery someday. But not this weekend. I need my sofa, my tea, and my book.

Maya: What about Sunday? Maybe we can do something together — just for a few hours?

Carlos: Oh! How about we go to the park? We can have a little picnic, maybe play frisbee or volleyball?

Lena: That’s not a bad idea. I could bring some sandwiches. But no running, okay?

Maya: Deal! I’ll bring fruit and drinks. And Carlos brings the games.

Carlos: Perfect! I’ll bring my speaker too — some music will make it more fun! Maybe even some salsa!

Lena: (Smiling) I guess I’m not staying home all weekend after all.

Maya: We all need a little fun and fresh air. It’s good for the brain!

Carlos: And the heart! What time works best for everyone?

Lena: Hmm... How about 11 in the morning?

Maya: Sounds good to me. Riverside Park?

Carlos: Yes! Sunday at 11 it is!

Lena: Wait — what will we do if it rains? The forecast said there might be showers.

Maya: Good point. If it rains, we’ll move the picnic to my apartment. I have a big living room.

Carlos: And if the rain stops early, we’ll still go to the park. I’ll check the forecast Sunday morning and text you both.

Lena: Should we invite anyone else? What about Priya from work? She’s new in town.

Maya: Great idea! I’ll ask her tomorrow. If she says yes, I’ll tell her to bring a dessert.

Carlos: Let’s make it a potluck then — everyone brings one dish. If we all bring something, we’ll have plenty of food.

Lena: I love that plan. I’m really looking forward to Sunday now — rain or shine!

Maya: Me too! Okay, so Sunday at 11, Riverside Park — or my place if it rains.

Carlos: (Raising his cup) To the best weekend ever!

Lena and Maya: (Yay!) To the weekend!

(They all smile and clink their coffee cups together.)

🎯 Speaking Challenge

  • Use 3 new vocabulary words in your conversation.
  • Talk about YOUR life, not just the characters'.
  • After you finish: your teacher will share useful corrections they heard — during the conversation, focus on speaking, not on being perfect.

Vocabulary List

  • Pottery /ˈpɑːtəri/ (n.) cups, bowls, and other objects made from clay. Example: Maya takes a pottery class every Saturday.
  • Picnic /ˈpɪknɪk/ (n.) a meal eaten outside, often in a park. Example: We had a picnic by the lake last summer.
  • Frisbee /ˈfrɪzbi/ (n.) a flat plastic disc thrown back and forth as a game. Example: Carlos will bring a frisbee to the park.
  • Forecast /ˈfɔːrkæst/ (n.) a report saying what the weather will probably be like. Example: The forecast says it might rain on Sunday.
  • Shower /ˈʃaʊər/ (n.) a short period of rain. Example: The showers stopped before noon.
  • Potluck /ˈpɑːtlʌk/ (n.) a meal where each person brings one dish to share. Example: Our office potluck had food from ten countries.
  • Clink /klɪŋk/ (v.) to make a short ringing sound when glass or metal hits together. Example: They clinked their coffee cups together.
  • Count me in /kaʊnt miː ɪn/ (phrase) include me; I want to join. Example: A picnic on Sunday? Count me in!
  • Look forward to /lʊk ˈfɔːrwərd tuː/ (phr v.) to feel excited about something that will happen. Example: I’m looking forward to seeing you.
  • Rain or shine /reɪn ɔːr ʃaɪn/ (idiom) no matter what the weather is; whatever happens. Example: The game will be played rain or shine.
