Calling the Utility Company

Setting: Tuesday afternoon. Maya just got the keys to her new apartment, and the movers come Saturday. She calls the electric company from her empty living room to turn on the electricity in time. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Maya — just moved to a new apartment; needs electricity turned on before the weekend.
  • Sam — a customer service representative at the electric company; friendly and patient.

Maya: (dialing the number) Okay, here goes. I need the electricity on before Saturday.

Sam: Thank you for calling City Power. This is Sam. How can I help you?

Maya: Hi, Sam. I just moved to a new apartment, and I need to get new service.

Sam: Welcome! I can help with that. First, what’s your full name?

Maya: Maya Torres.

Sam: Thanks, Maya. And what’s the service address?

Maya: 456 Oak Street, Apartment 3B, Los Angeles.

Sam: Got it. What’s a good phone number for the account?

Maya: 213-555-0147.

Sam: Perfect. Now, when would you like the service turned on?

Maya: This Friday, if possible. The movers come Saturday morning.

Sam: Friday works fine. Would you like a paperless bill?

Maya: What’s a paperless bill, exactly?

Sam: It means we email your bill instead of mailing a paper one. It’s free, and it arrives faster.

Maya: That sounds good. Yes, please. How much is the total amount due for the first month?

Sam: There’s a $25 setup fee plus a $50 deposit. Your first bill will show the total amount due.

Maya: A deposit? Do I get that money back?

Sam: Yes — after twelve months of on-time payments, we return it to you.

Maya: Okay. What’s the billing date? I want to plan my budget.

Sam: Your billing date is the 5th of each month, and the payment due date is the 20th.

Maya: Great. And who do I call if I have a problem with my bill?

Sam: Call us anytime. Just have your account number ready — I’ll email it to you today.

Maya: Perfect. One more question — what happens if I move again next year?

Sam: Just call us. We’ll cancel your existing service here and set it up at the new address.

Maya: That’s everything. Thank you so much, Sam!

Sam: You’re welcome, Maya. Welcome to City Power!

🎯 Speaking Challenge

  • Use 3 new vocabulary words in your conversation.
  • Talk about YOUR life, not just the characters'.
  • After you finish: your teacher will share useful corrections they heard — during the conversation, focus on speaking, not on being perfect.

Vocabulary List

  • Utilities /juˈtɪlɪtiz/ (n.) services like electricity, gas, and water for your home. Example: The apartment includes all utilities except electricity.
  • Landlord /ˈlændlɔrd/ (n.) the person who owns the apartment or house you rent. Example: Our landlord fixed the broken heater.
  • Account number /əˈkaʊnt ˈnʌmbər/ (n.) the special number for your bill or service account. Example: You need your account number to pay online.
  • Billing date /ˈbɪlɪŋ deɪt/ (n.) the day the company makes your bill each month. Example: My billing date is the 5th.
  • Total amount due /ˈtoʊtəl əˈmaʊnt du/ (n.) all the money you must pay on a bill. Example: The total amount due is $87.50.
  • Payment due date /ˈpeɪmənt du deɪt/ (n.) the last day you can pay a bill. Example: The payment due date is the 20th.
  • Turn on /tɜrn ɑn/ (v. phrase) to start a service, like electricity or gas. Example: We need to turn on the electricity before Friday.
  • Customer service representative /ˈkʌstəmər ˈsɜrvɪs ˌrɛprɪˈzɛntətɪv/ (n.) a person who helps customers by phone or chat. Example: The customer service representative answered my questions.
  • Paperless bill /ˈpeɪpərləs bɪl/ (n.) a bill you get by email, not on paper. Example: I get a paperless bill every month.
  • Cancel existing service /ˈkænsəl ɪɡˈzɪstɪŋ ˈsɜrvɪs/ (v. phrase) to stop a service you already have. Example: She called to cancel existing service at her old apartment.
