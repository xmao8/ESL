House Hunting

Setting: Sunday afternoon at a coffee shop. Maya has to move out of her apartment in three weeks, and she is stressed about finding a new place. Her friend Sam, who just moved last month, is helping her compare listings on her phone. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Maya — needs a new apartment soon; wants more space but has a tight budget.
  • Sam — Maya's friend; just finished his own apartment hunt and loves giving advice.

Maya: I can't believe I have to move in three weeks. I haven't found anything yet!

Sam: Relax. I was in the same panic last month, and it worked out. What are you looking for?

Maya: Something with more space than my current place. My studio is so small I can touch both walls at once.

Sam: (Laughs) Okay, so a one-bedroom at least. What's your budget?

Maya: Around $1,600 a month. And I really want a balcony this time.

Sam: A balcony is nice, but it usually means higher rent. Are you willing to pay more for one?

Maya: Maybe a little more. I want somewhere I can drink coffee in the morning.

Sam: Fair. Look at this listing: one-bedroom, spacious living room, big windows, $1,550. The location is convenient too — near the train.

Maya: "Convenient location" — that means close to everything, right? But does it have parking? I have a car now.

Sam: It says "carport included." That's like a covered parking spot, but with open sides. Better than street parking.

Maya: Hmm. What about this one? It's charming — brick walls, high ceilings — and only $1,450.

Sam: Charming usually means old. Check the appliances. Old buildings sometimes have the original stove from 1970.

Maya: Good point. It says "new refrigerator" but nothing about the washer and dryer.

Sam: Always ask. I toured a place with the most beautiful kitchen, and then found out there was no washer and dryer at all.

Maya: Ugh, I hate laundromats. Okay, next: a condo in a gated community with a pool and tennis courts. $1,700.

Sam: Over budget — and condos in gated communities often have extra monthly fees. Did you check?

Maya: You're right, there's a $150 fee. So it's really $1,850. Too much.

Sam: What matters most to you — space, location, or price? You can't have the most of everything.

Maya: Honestly? A quiet place with air conditioning. My studio has no AC and last summer was terrible.

Sam: Then skip anything without it. Here's one: two-bedroom, air conditioning, bathtub, $1,600. A little farther out, though.

Maya: A bathtub! I haven't had a real bath in years. But "farther out" — how far?

Sam: Twenty minutes by train. Less convenient, but you get more rooms for the money.

Maya: And the security deposit? Some places ask for two months. That's a lot of cash up front.

Sam: This one asks for one month. That's normal. Just make sure you get it back — take photos when you move in.

🎯 Speaking Challenge

  • Use 3 new vocabulary words in your conversation.
  • Talk about YOUR life, not just the characters'.
  • After you finish: your teacher will share useful corrections they heard — during the conversation, focus on speaking, not on being perfect.

Vocabulary List

  • Studio /ˈstudiˌoʊ/ (n.) a small apartment with one main room for living and sleeping. Example: Her first apartment was a tiny studio downtown.
  • Spacious /ˈspeɪʃəs/ (adj.) big, with a lot of open space. Example: The living room is spacious enough for a sofa and a desk.
  • Security deposit /sɪˈkjʊrəti dɪˈpɑzɪt/ (n.) money paid before moving in, returned later if nothing is damaged. Example: The landlord asked for a $1,000 security deposit.
  • Convenient location /kənˈvinjənt loʊˈkeɪʃən/ (n. phrase) a place that is close to the things you need. Example: The apartment's convenient location saves me an hour a day.
  • Balcony /ˈbælkəni/ (n.) a small outdoor platform attached to an apartment, above the ground. Example: They eat breakfast on the balcony every morning.
  • Appliances /əˈplaɪənsɪz/ (n. pl.) machines in a home such as a stove, refrigerator, or washer. Example: The new appliances come with the apartment.
  • Charming /ˈtʃɑrmɪŋ/ (adj.) pleasant and attractive in a warm, old-fashioned way. Example: They live in a charming house with a red door.
  • Gated community /ˈɡeɪtɪd kəˈmjunəti/ (n.) a neighborhood with a gate and guard controlling who enters. Example: The gated community has a pool and a playground.
  • Bathtub /ˈbæθtʌb/ (n.) a large container in a bathroom that you fill with water to wash your body. Example: After work, she relaxed in a hot bathtub.
  • Air conditioning /ɛr kənˈdɪʃənɪŋ/ (n.) a system that keeps indoor air cool. Example: Make sure the air conditioning works before summer.
