Benefits & Workplace Safety

Setting: Monday morning at a warehouse. Marco is a new worker, and Ms. Chen, the safety supervisor, shows him the safety gear and explains the job benefits. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Marco — a new worker at a warehouse; today is his first day, and he wants to learn the safety rules.
  • Ms. Chen — the safety supervisor at the warehouse; she shows Marco the safety gear and explains the job benefits.

Dialogue: At the Warehouse

Ms. Chen: Good morning, Marco. Welcome to the warehouse. Today I will show you the safety rules. First, your safety gear — this hard hat and these safety goggles. If you don't wear a hard hat, you might get hurt. If you forget your safety goggles, you could hurt your eyes.

Marco: Thank you. I will wear them every day. What happens if the floor is wet?

Ms. Chen: If the floor is wet, you might slip and fall. We clean up spills at once — if you see water, tell me.

Marco: What about these heavy boxes? They look very heavy.

Ms. Chen: If you lift a heavy box the wrong way, you could hurt your back. Bend your knees, keep your back straight, and ask for help.

Marco: I understand. Where is the first aid kit?

Ms. Chen: It is on the wall by the door. If someone gets hurt, you could find bandages and medicine there.

Marco: Thank you. What should I do if I feel sick?

Ms. Chen: If you feel sick, you could ask me for help, and you can use your paid sick days. Your health comes first.

Marco: That's good to know. What benefits does this job offer?

Ms. Chen: We offer health insurance and dental insurance. If you need a doctor, your health insurance helps pay for the visit.

Marco: What about time off? I have a baby at home.

Ms. Chen: You have paid vacation days and paid personal days. If your baby is sick, you could take family leave. And if you need surgery, you could take medical leave.

Marco: What is a 401K? I saw it on the job ad.

Ms. Chen: A 401K helps you save money for retirement. If you put money in every month, the company adds money too.

Marco: And what about overtime? If I work extra hours, what happens?

Ms. Chen: If you work overtime, you get overtime pay. And if you do good work, you might get a bonus at the end of the year.

Marco: What if I get hurt and cannot work? And what about daycare for my child?

Ms. Chen: We have disability insurance — if you are hurt at work, it gives you money while you get better. We don't have daycare here, but if you need maternity leave, talk to me and I can help you with the forms.

Marco: Thank you, Ms. Chen. If I follow the rules, I could stay safe — and if I ask questions, I might learn faster.

Ms. Chen: Exactly. If you keep learning, you could become a great worker here. Let's start.

🗣️ Speaking challenge — role play (about 5 minutes)

  • Student A is the supervisor. Show a new worker the safety rules at your workplace. Use if-sentences with could and might: “If you don't wear a hard hat, you might get hurt.”
  • Student B is the new worker. Ask three questions about safety or benefits. Use could or might in each question: “If I feel sick, could I use my paid sick days?”
  • Then swap roles and try a different workplace: a restaurant kitchen, a construction site, or a hospital.
  • Use at least three could / might if-sentences in your talk. Use at least three of the words below.

Vocabulary List

  • 401K /fɔːr oʊ wʌn keɪ/ (n.) money you save for retirement; your job can help you save it. Example: A 401K helps you save money for retirement.
  • Bonus /ˈboʊnəs/ (n.) extra money for good work. Example: If you do good work, you might get a bonus.
  • Dental insurance /ˈdentəl ɪnˈʃʊrəns/ (n.) insurance that pays for visits to the dentist. Example: Dental insurance pays for visits to the dentist.
  • Disability insurance /ˌdɪsəˈbɪləti ɪnˈʃʊrəns/ (n.) money you get if you cannot work because of an injury. Example: Disability insurance gives you money if you are hurt at work.
  • Maternity leave /məˈtɜːrnəti liːv/ (n.) time off work after a baby is born. Example: A mother can take maternity leave after her baby is born.
  • Health insurance /helθ ɪnˈʃʊrəns/ (n.) insurance that helps pay for doctor visits and medicine. Example: Health insurance helps pay for a visit to the doctor.
  • Daycare /ˈdeɪker/ (n.) a place where workers can leave their young children during the day. Example: Some companies have a daycare for workers' children.
  • Family leave /ˈfæməli liːv/ (n.) time off work to care for a sick family member. Example: You can take family leave if your baby is sick.
  • Medical leave /ˈmedɪkəl liːv/ (n.) time off work for surgery or medical treatment. Example: If you need surgery, you can take medical leave.
  • Overtime /ˈoʊvərtaɪm/ (n.) extra hours of work; also the extra pay for those hours. Example: If you work overtime, you get overtime pay.
  • Paid personal days /peɪd ˈpɜːrsənəl deɪz/ (n.) days off you can use for a personal reason, with pay. Example: You can use paid personal days to take a day off.
  • Paid sick days /peɪd sɪk deɪz/ (n.) days off when you are sick, with pay. Example: If you feel sick, use your paid sick days.
  • Paid vacation days /peɪd vəˈkeɪʃən deɪz/ (n.) days off for travel or rest, with pay. Example: Workers use paid vacation days in the summer.
  • Hard hat /hɑːrd hæt/ (n.) a strong hat that protects your head at work. Example: A construction worker wears a hard hat.
  • Safety goggles /ˈseɪfti ˈɡɑɡəlz/ (n.) glasses that protect your eyes at work. Example: Wear safety goggles to protect your eyes.
