Asking for Directions Downtown

Characters:

  • Lucas: A visitor downtown with three errands and only two hours.
  • Nina: A friendly local who knows every street downtown.

Scene: Mei is new in town and trying to find the public library. Her phone died, so she asks Ben, a passerby, for directions.

Conversation

LUCAS: Excuse me — do you know this area? I have three errands and only two hours!

NINA: I do! I live right downtown. What do you need to do?

LUCAS: First, I need to pick up my dry cleaning. Is there a dry cleaner near here?

NINA: Yes — go straight for two blocks, then turn left on Fifth Street. It's next to the bank.

LUCAS: Got it. After I pick up the dry cleaning, I need to mail a package. Where is the post office?

NINA: The post office is across from the park. Take Broadway to Sixth Avenue, and it's on the corner.

LUCAS: Is it far from the dry cleaner?

NINA: Not really — about a ten-minute walk. When you leave the dry cleaner, just follow Fifth Street back to Broadway.

LUCAS: Perfect. And last, I need to go grocery shopping before I go home.

NINA: There's a big store two blocks from the post office. When you finish at the post office, turn right and walk straight.

LUCAS: Should I walk, or take the bus?

NINA: Walking is fine — everything is close. But if you're tired, taking the bus is easy too. The bus stop is in front of the post office.

LUCAS: Good to know. What time does the post office close?

NINA: At five. So mail your package before you go grocery shopping!

LUCAS: Right — post office first, then groceries. When I finish shopping, I'll call a ride share app.

NINA: Smart plan. The parking lot behind the store is a good pickup spot.

LUCAS: You've saved my afternoon. Before I go — is there a good restaurant around here?

NINA: Yes! There's a great taco place across from the theater. After your errands, you should try it.

LUCAS: Tacos after errands — the perfect ending. Thank you so much!

NINA: You're welcome! Good luck with your errands!

Vocabulary List

  • errand /ˈɛrənd/ (n.) a short trip to do something useful. Example: I have three errands today.
  • dry cleaning /draɪ ˈklinɪŋ/ (n.) clothes cleaned at a special store. Example: I need to pick up my dry cleaning.
  • post office /poʊst ˈɔfɪs/ (n.) the place where you mail letters and packages. Example: The post office closes at five.
  • across from /əˈkrɔs frʌm/ (phr.) on the other side of the street. Example: The post office is across from the park.
  • on the corner /ɑn ðə ˈkɔrnər/ (phr.) where two streets meet. Example: It's on the corner of Broadway and Sixth.
  • ride share app /raɪd ʃɛr æp/ (n.) a phone app for car rides, like Uber. Example: I'll call a ride share app.
  • bus stop /bʌs stɑp/ (n.) the place where the bus picks you up. Example: The bus stop is in front of the post office.
  • parking lot /ˈpɑrkɪŋ lɑt/ (n.) an area for parking cars. Example: The parking lot is behind the store.
  • turn left /tɜrn lɛft/ (phr.) to go in the left direction. Example: Turn left on Fifth Street.
  • go straight /ɡoʊ streɪt/ (phr.) to continue without turning. Example: Go straight for two blocks.
