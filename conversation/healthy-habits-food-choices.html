Healthy Habits & Food Choices

Setting: Monday lunch in the office break room. Elena brings a homemade lunch, and Marco brings fast food again. They talk about old habits, food labels, and one small change each. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Habit /ˈhæbɪt/ (n.) something you do often, usually without thinking. Example: Drinking water in the morning is a good habit.
  • Nutrition /nuːˈtrɪʃən/ (n.) the food and drink your body needs to stay healthy. Example: Good nutrition gives you energy for the day.
  • Serving size /ˈsɜːrvɪŋ saɪz/ (n.) how much food you eat at one time. Example: The serving size on this box is one cup.
  • Calories /ˈkæləriz/ (n.) a measure of the energy in food. Example: This snack has 150 calories.
  • Fiber /ˈfaɪbər/ (n.) the part of plant foods that helps digestion. Example: Beans and whole grains have a lot of fiber.
  • Balanced diet /ˈbælənst ˈdaɪət/ (n.) eating foods from all the food groups. Example: A balanced diet has vegetables, fruit, grains, protein, and dairy.
  • Craving /ˈkreɪvɪŋ/ (n.) a strong wish to eat something. Example: I have a craving for something sweet after dinner.
  • Snack /snæk/ (n.) a small amount of food between meals. Example: An apple is a healthy snack.
  • Whole grain /hoʊl ɡreɪn/ (n.) a grain that still has all its natural parts, like brown rice. Example: Whole grain bread is better than white bread.
  • Skip /skɪp/ (v.) to not eat a meal or not do something. Example: Do not skip breakfast before class.
