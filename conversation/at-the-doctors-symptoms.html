At the Doctor: Describing Symptoms

Setting: Monday morning at a doctor's office. Luis feels sick, so he visits Dr. Park and describes his symptoms. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Luis — a patient; he has a fever, a sore throat, and a cough.
  • Dr. Park — a kind doctor who listens, checks, and gives advice.

Luis: Good morning, Dr. Park. Thank you for seeing me today.

Dr. Park: Good morning, Luis. What brings you in today?

Luis: I don't feel well. I have a fever and a sore throat.

Dr. Park: I'm sorry to hear that. How long have you felt sick?

Luis: Since yesterday morning. My throat hurts when I swallow.

Dr. Park: Do you have a cough or a headache, too?

Luis: Yes, a small cough. No headache, but I feel very tired.

Dr. Park: Let me check your throat and your temperature. Please open your mouth.

Luis: Ahh. Is it serious, doctor?

Dr. Park: No, it looks like a common cold. You need rest and lots of water.

Luis: Should I take any medicine?

Dr. Park: Yes. I will give you a prescription for your cough. Take it to the pharmacy.

Luis: How often should I take it?

Dr. Park: Three times a day, after meals. And rest at home for two days.

Luis: Can I go to work tomorrow?

Dr. Park: No, stay home tomorrow. Come back if your fever gets worse.

Luis: Thank you, doctor. When should I make my next appointment?

Dr. Park: If you feel better in three days, you don't need one. Take care, Luis.

Luis: Thank you! Goodbye, Dr. Park.

Dr. Park: Goodbye, and get well soon!

🎯 Speaking Challenge

  • Use 3 new vocabulary words in your conversation.
  • Describe YOUR symptoms: what hurts, how long, and what helps.
  • Switch roles: one person is the doctor, the other is the patient. Talk about YOUR life, not just the characters'.

Vocabulary List

  • Fever /ˈfiːvər/ (n.) a body temperature that is higher than normal. Example: I have a fever, so I stayed home today.
  • Sore throat /sɔːr θroʊt/ (n.) pain in the throat, often when you swallow. Example: My sore throat hurts when I drink water.
  • Headache /ˈhedeɪk/ (n.) pain in the head. Example: I have a headache, so I need to rest.
  • Stomachache /ˈstʌməkeɪk/ (n.) pain in the stomach. Example: He ate too fast and now he has a stomachache.
  • Cough /kɔːf/ (n.) a sudden push of air out of the throat, often when you are sick. Example: Her cough kept her awake last night.
  • Prescription /prɪˈskrɪpʃən/ (n.) a doctor's written order for medicine. Example: The doctor gave me a prescription for my cough.
  • Pharmacy /ˈfɑːrməsi/ (n.) the store where you get medicine. Example: Take this prescription to the pharmacy.
  • Appointment /əˈpɔɪntmənt/ (n.) a time you agree to meet a doctor. Example: I made an appointment for Friday morning.
  • Symptoms /ˈsɪmptəmz/ (n.) signs that show you are sick, like a fever or a cough. Example: Tell the doctor all your symptoms.
  • Rest /rest/ (v.) to relax and sleep so your body can get better. Example: You should rest and drink lots of water.
