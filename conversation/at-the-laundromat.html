At the Laundromat

Characters:

  • JAMES: The attendant at a neighborhood laundromat, patient and friendly.
  • AISHA: A nursing student doing laundry outside her home for the first time.

Scene: Aisha just moved into an apartment without a washer and dryer. It’s her first time at a laundromat. James, the attendant, helps her.

Conversation

AISHA: Excuse me — is this your first time here too, or do you work here?

JAMES: I work here! I’m James, the attendant. Is this your first time at a laundromat?

AISHA: Yes. My apartment doesn’t have a washer, and I have no idea how these machines work.

JAMES: No worries — it’s easy once you’ve done it once. First, separate your clothes: whites in one machine, colors in another.

AISHA: Okay. How much does one load cost?

JAMES: The large washers are $4.50 per load. The dryers are 25 cents for every 7 minutes.

AISHA: Do the machines take cash, or do I need a card?

JAMES: Both. You can use coins, or you can load money onto a laundry card at that machine by the door.

AISHA: I think I’ll get a laundry card. It sounds more convenient than carrying quarters.

JAMES: Good choice. Most regulars use the card. Now — do you have detergent?

AISHA: Detergent… that’s the soap, right? I forgot to buy some!

JAMES: That’s okay. We sell single-use packets right here for $1.50. You’ll also want dryer sheets for the dryer.

AISHA: What do dryer sheets do?

JAMES: They reduce static — you know, when your clothes stick together — and they make everything smell fresh.

AISHA: Got it. How long does a wash cycle take?

JAMES: About 30 minutes for a normal cycle. Then move your clothes to a dryer for 40 to 50 minutes.

AISHA: Should I stay here the whole time, or can I leave and come back?

JAMES: Most people leave and come back. Just don’t leave your clothes unattended for too long — someone might move them.

AISHA: Good tip. Is there Wi-Fi here? I could study while I wait.

JAMES: There is! The password is on the sign by the folding tables.

AISHA: This is much less scary than I thought. Thank you for explaining everything, James.

JAMES: Anytime, Aisha. After today, you’ll be a laundromat expert!

— THE END —

Vocabulary List

  • Laundromat /ˈlɔːndrəmæt/ (n.) a store with washers and dryers you pay to use. Example: It’s her first time at a laundromat.
  • Load /loʊd/ (n.) the amount of clothes washed at one time. Example: How much does one load cost?
  • Separate /ˈsepəreɪt/ (v.) to divide into different groups. Example: Separate your clothes: whites and colors.
  • Detergent /dɪˈtɜːrdʒənt/ (n.) soap made for washing clothes. Example: Do you have detergent?
  • Dryer sheet /ˈdraɪər ʃiːt/ (n.) a thin sheet that reduces static in the dryer. Example: You’ll want dryer sheets.
  • Static /ˈstætɪk/ (n.) electricity that makes clothes stick together. Example: They reduce static.
  • Wash cycle /wɑːʃ ˈsaɪkəl/ (n.) one complete run of a washing machine. Example: A wash cycle takes 30 minutes.
  • Unattended /ˌʌnəˈtendɪd/ (adj.) not watched or supervised. Example: Don’t leave your clothes unattended.
  • Folding table /ˈfoʊldɪŋ ˈteɪbəl/ (n.) a table for folding clean clothes. Example: The password is by the folding tables.
  • Attendant /əˈtendənt/ (n.) a worker who helps customers at a business. Example: James is the attendant.
