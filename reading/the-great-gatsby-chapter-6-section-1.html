The Great Gatsby — Chapter VI, Section 1: Before Jay Gatsby (Abridged Reader)

Adapted from F. Scott Fitzgerald, The Great Gatsby (1925), in the U.S. public domain. Read the original at gutenberg.org/ebooks/64317.

One morning a young reporter from New York appeared at Gatsby’s door. He asked whether Gatsby had anything to say. Gatsby politely asked what he was supposed to speak about. The reporter had heard the name at work, but he would not or could not explain why it mattered. It was his day off and he had come to see what he might find. There were so many stories about Gatsby by then that it was almost news simply to stand at his door.

People said there was a secret pipeline to Canada or that his mansion was really a boat that could be moved along the shore in the dark. These stories grew as people carried them away from the parties. The truth was strange in a quieter way. Gatsby had told it to me much later, when I hardly knew what I believed about him. I am putting it here because this is a good place to understand who he had been before the rumors began.

His name was really James Gatz. His parents were poor farmers in North Dakota. At seventeen he had already imagined a different person and given that person a new name. For a while he lived along the shore of Lake Superior. He dug for clams, fished, and took whatever work would earn him dinner and a bed. At night his mind was busy building a brilliant future. He went to St. Olaf’s, a small college in Minnesota, but left after two weeks. The college had no idea of the great things he expected from life, and he hated doing cleaning work to pay his way.

Then he saw a yacht anchored above a dangerous shallow part of the lake. Dan Cody, its owner, had made a fortune in mining, especially in copper. He had traveled on that yacht for years. Young Gatz borrowed a rowboat, went out to the deck, and warned Cody that the wind could drive the yacht into trouble. The boy on the beach had worn a torn green shirt. The boy who spoke to the millionaire introduced himself as Jay Gatsby. I think he had been ready to use the name for some time.

To the boy in the rowboat, the yacht seemed to hold everything beautiful and exciting in the world. Cody thought the young man was quick and ambitious. A few days later he took him to Duluth and bought him a coat, white trousers, and a cap for sailing. When the yacht went away, Gatsby went with it.

Over the next five years Gatsby took on several jobs for Cody. At times he looked after the yacht, at times he acted as secretary, and at times Cody trusted him to keep an eye on him when he was drinking. Watching Cody helped Gatsby decide not to drink much himself. It was not the kind of education his later guests could imagine, but it taught him how a rich man lived and what wealth could do to him.

A woman named Ella Kaye came aboard one night in Boston. Cody died a week later. He had left Gatsby twenty-five thousand dollars, but Gatsby never received it. He never understood the legal arrangement that kept it from him, and Ella Kaye received the rest of the fortune. If Gatsby did not come away rich, at least he came away knowing what the life he wanted looked like up close. He had invented himself as a boy. After those years with Cody, Jay Gatsby had become a person who could walk into a room and make others believe the invention.

Vocabulary List

  • reporter /rɪˈpɔrtər/ — A person whose job is to write news stories.
  • pipeline /ˈpaɪplaɪn/ — A long pipe that carries oil or gas.
  • brilliant /ˈbrɪljənt/ — Very bright, or very clever and wonderful.
  • anchored /ˈæŋkərd/ — Held in place in the water by a heavy anchor.
  • fortune /ˈfɔrtʃən/ — A large amount of money.
  • copper /ˈkɑpər/ — A reddish metal used to make coins and wires.
  • ambitious /æmˈbɪʃəs/ — Wanting very much to be successful.
  • secretary /ˈsekrəteri/ — A person who helps someone with letters and office work.
  • arrangement /əˈreɪndʒmənt/ — A plan or agreement about how something will be done.
  • invented /ɪnˈventɪd/ — Made up something that did not exist before.
  • imagined /ɪˈmædʒɪnd/ — Formed a picture of something in your mind.
  • clams /klæmz/ — Small shellfish that live in sand near the sea.
