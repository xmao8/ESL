A New Fitness Routine

Setting: Saturday morning at a neighborhood park. Rosa has just finished a morning walk, and her friend Daniel is thinking about changing careers — he is curious about health jobs, but he is not sure where to start. They talk about fitness routines, healthy habits, and jobs that help people stay healthy. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Rosa — walks every morning, goes to a gym class twice a week, and wants a stronger routine.
  • Daniel — works in an office, sits most of the day, and is thinking about a career in health or fitness.

Rosa: Daniel! You are up early. I thought you slept until noon on Saturdays.

Daniel: Not today. I decided to start a fitness routine, and I wanted to ask you how you do it. You always look so full of energy.

Rosa: I just walk every morning. Thirty minutes, before breakfast. It is simple, and it wakes me up.

Daniel: Thirty minutes every day? That sounds like a lot. What if I get tired after ten minutes?

Rosa: Then walk ten minutes! A short walk is better than no walk. You can add five minutes each week.

Daniel: That sounds possible. Do you stretch before you walk?

Rosa: Yes, a quick warm-up and a little stretching. It helps my legs feel ready, and I do not get sore.

Daniel: I should try that. My back hurts from sitting at my desk all day.

Rosa: Sitting all day is hard on the body. A physical therapist once told my uncle to stand up and move every hour.

Daniel: A physical therapist? That is one of the health careers I have been reading about.

Rosa: Really? Are you thinking about a new career?

Daniel: Maybe. I like helping people, but I do not want to sit at a desk forever. If I studied health, I could become a trainer or a nurse.

Rosa: If you became a trainer, you would help people plan workouts. That sounds like a fun job.

Daniel: It does. But trainers need to know a lot about the body. What if I am not strong enough myself?

Rosa: Trainers do not need to be the strongest person in the gym. They need to listen, explain clearly, and keep people safe.

Daniel: That is true. What about a dietitian? I like cooking, and food is interesting to me.

Rosa: A dietitian plans healthy meals for people. My cousin sees one — she learned how to read food labels and plan balanced meals.

Daniel: I read food labels too, but I do not always understand them. Sugar is in everything!

Rosa: That is why nutrition matters. Small changes help: more water, more vegetables, less fast food.

Daniel: I already drink more water this week. One good habit at a time, right?

Rosa: Exactly. And sleep matters too. If I sleep less than seven hours, I feel tired all day.

Daniel: Same here. So, will you walk with me tomorrow morning? I need someone to keep me honest.

Rosa: Of course! Meet me here at eight. Bring water, wear comfortable shoes, and we will start slow.

Daniel: Deal. And if I enjoy it, maybe I will join your gym class next week.

Rosa: You should! The trainer there is friendly, and beginners are welcome. One step at a time — for fitness and for your career.

🎯 Speaking Challenge

  • Use 3 new vocabulary words in your conversation.
  • Talk about YOUR life, not just the characters'.
  • After you finish: your teacher will share useful corrections they heard — during the conversation, focus on speaking, not on being perfect.

Vocabulary List

  • Workout /ˈwɜrkˌaʊt/ (n.) a set of exercises you do to stay strong and healthy. Example: Her morning workout is a walk and some stretching.
  • Warm-up /ˈwɔrmˌʌp/ (n.) gentle movement that prepares your body for exercise. Example: Do a five-minute warm-up before you run.
  • Stretch /strɛtʃ/ (v.) to reach your arms, legs, or back out slowly to loosen your muscles. Example: Stretch your legs after a long walk.
  • Trainer /ˈtreɪnər/ (n.) a person who helps others plan exercise and stay safe. Example: The trainer showed me how to lift weights correctly.
  • Nutrition /nuˈtrɪʃən/ (n.) the food your body needs to stay healthy. Example: Good nutrition means more vegetables and less sugar.
  • Dietitian /ˌdaɪəˈtɪʃən/ (n.) a health worker who plans healthy meals for people. Example: The dietitian helped him plan meals for his busy week.
  • Physical therapist /ˈfɪzɪkəl ˈθɛrəpɪst/ (n.) a health worker who helps people move better after an injury. Example: A physical therapist helped her walk again after the accident.
  • Symptom /ˈsɪmptəm/ (n.) a sign that you may be sick, like a cough or a headache. Example: A fever is a common symptom of the flu.
  • Appointment /əˈpɔɪntmənt/ (n.) a time you arrange to see a doctor or another worker. Example: Call the clinic to make an appointment for Monday.
  • Healthy habit /ˈhɛlθi ˈhæbɪt/ (n.) something good you do often, like walking daily or drinking water. Example: Sleeping eight hours is a healthy habit.
