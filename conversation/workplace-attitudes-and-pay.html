A New Manager and Payday: Talking About Work

Setting: Friday afternoon in the hotel break room. Rosa and Kevin are coworkers at a hotel. They talk about their new manager, Mr. Chen, and their paychecks. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Rosa — works at the hotel front desk; she wants to become a manager one day.
  • Kevin — works in the hotel restaurant; he is quiet, but his work is excellent.

Rosa: Kevin, did you see our new manager? His name is Mr. Chen, and his office is next to mine.

Kevin: Yes, I met him this morning. He seems serious, but his smile is friendly.

Rosa: I agree. He is strict about time, but he is patient with new workers. That is good — my first manager was very demanding.

Kevin: Mine was too! My old manager wanted everything perfect, and his rules were hard to follow.

Rosa: Mr. Chen is different. He told us, “Your ideas are important. Our hotel is your hotel too.”

Kevin: I like that. He is courteous to everyone — he says “please” and “thank you” to all his workers. Ours is a lucky team!

Rosa: And he is funny! Did you hear his joke at the meeting? The joke was his — he wrote it himself!

Kevin: Yes! A funny manager is better than a quiet one. Our old manager never talked to us.

Rosa: True. But is Mr. Chen ambitious? I think he wants our hotel to be the best in the city. He says our hotel and its reputation are his top priority.

Kevin: He is. He told me his plan: better training, better pay, and more respect for workers.

Rosa: Speaking of pay — did you get your paycheck? Mine came yesterday. My gross pay was $1,600, but my net pay was only $1,300.

Kevin: Mine was the same. Our earnings look good on paper, but theirs are higher — the managers earn more than us!

Rosa: I know. I checked my pay stub. My gross pay is $1,600, and my net pay is $1,300. Maria got hers yesterday — hers was $1,450!

Kevin: Almost. Yours is a little higher than mine because your hours are longer. My hours are shorter this month.

Rosa: That is fair. You are a hardworking person, Kevin. Your work is excellent, and the guests love you.

Kevin: Thank you, Rosa. You are respectful to everyone, and your English is getting better every day. Maria told me her English class is helping her.

Rosa: Thanks! My goal is to become a manager one day. I want to learn everything about this hotel.

Kevin: You will. Your attitude is interesting — you ask good questions, and you help your coworkers.

Rosa: And you are quiet, but your work speaks for itself. The guests love their rooms and always ask for you!

Kevin: Ha! Well, our payday is Friday, and our manager is good. I think our jobs are safe with Mr. Chen.

🎯 Speaking Challenge

  • Use 3 new vocabulary words in your conversation.
  • Answer as YOURSELF: describe your manager with 2 adjectives (strict? friendly? funny?) and talk about your pay. Use possessive adjectives (my, your, our) and possessive pronouns (mine, yours, ours).
  • Switch roles: one person is Rosa, the other is Kevin. Then talk about YOUR life, not just the characters'.

Vocabulary List

  • Ambitious /æmˈbɪʃəs/ (adj.) wanting to do better and reach a higher job. Example: Maria is ambitious — she wants to become a manager one day.
  • Courteous /ˈkɜːrtəs/ (adj.) polite; saying “please” and “thank you”. Example: Our manager is courteous to everyone at work.
  • Demanding /dɪˈmændɪŋ/ (adj.) wanting a lot from workers; hard to please. Example: My old manager was demanding — he wanted everything perfect.
  • Friendly /ˈfrendli/ (adj.) kind and warm to other people. Example: Rosa is friendly and smiles at all her coworkers.
  • Funny /ˈfʌni/ (adj.) making people laugh. Example: Our new manager is funny — his jokes make everyone laugh.
  • Hardworking /ˈhɑːrdˈwɜːrkɪŋ/ (adj.) working hard; never stopping until the job is done. Example: Kevin is hardworking — the guests always ask for him.
  • Interesting /ˈɪntrəstɪŋ/ (adj.) holding your attention; making you want to learn more. Example: Your attitude is interesting — you ask good questions.
  • Patient /ˈpeɪʃənt/ (adj.) able to wait calmly without getting angry. Example: A good manager is patient with new workers.
  • Quiet /ˈkwaɪət/ (adj.) not talking much; making little noise. Example: Tom is quiet and does not talk much at work.
  • Respectful /rɪˈspektfəl/ (adj.) showing respect; listening and following the rules. Example: A respectful worker listens to the manager.
  • Serious /ˈsɪriəs/ (adj.) not joking; thinking carefully about work. Example: Mr. Chen seems serious, but his smile is friendly.
  • Strict /strɪkt/ (adj.) having many rules; checking everything. Example: Our manager is strict about time.
  • Earnings /ˈɜːrnɪŋz/ (n.) the money you make from your job. Example: Your earnings are the money you make from your job.
  • Gross pay /ɡroʊs peɪ/ (n.) your pay before taxes. Example: My gross pay is $1,600, but my net pay is $1,300.
  • Net pay /net peɪ/ (n.) the money you take home after taxes. Example: My net pay is the money I take home after taxes.
