The Great Gatsby — Chapter IV, Section 3: Jordan’s Story (Abridged Reader)

Adapted from F. Scott Fitzgerald, The Great Gatsby (1925), in the U.S. public domain. Read the original at gutenberg.org/ebooks/64317.

That afternoon I met Jordan for tea at the Plaza Hotel. She began her story in Louisville, on an October day in 1917. Daisy Fay was eighteen then, two years older than Jordan and admired by nearly everyone. Young officers from the nearby army camp called her all day. That morning, Jordan found Daisy in her little white car with a lieutenant. He looked at Daisy so intently that he seemed to see no one else. Jordan never forgot the look. The officer’s name was Jay Gatsby.

Jordan did not see him again for years. After he and Daisy had met, there was a rumor that Daisy’s mother had caught her packing a bag to go to New York to say goodbye to a soldier leaving the country. Her family prevented the trip, and for weeks she would hardly speak to them. Later she went out again and seemed as cheerful as before. In June she married Tom Buchanan. The wedding drew the attention of all Louisville. Before it took place, he gave her a string of very expensive pearls.

Jordan was a bridesmaid. Just before the wedding dinner, she found Daisy on her bed with a bottle of wine in one hand and a letter in the other. Daisy had been drinking, which was unusual for her. She pulled the pearls out of a wastebasket and asked Jordan to give them back. She had changed her mind, she said. Then she began to cry. Jordan and a maid tried to calm her and put her in a cold bath. Daisy held the letter so tightly that it became a wet ball. She gave it up only when the paper began to fall apart.

Half an hour later, the pearls were around her neck again. The next day Daisy married Tom without showing the least sign of doubt. When Jordan saw them in Santa Barbara after their honeymoon, Daisy seemed devoted to her husband. She looked for him as soon as he left the room. Not long afterward, though, Tom wrecked his car and injured a woman who was riding with him, a hotel employee. By the next spring Daisy had a daughter. The family went to France for a time and later lived in Chicago.

Jordan now knew that the Gatsby of West Egg was that officer. She finally did so after a dinner at the Buchanans’ house, when Daisy came into her room and asked which Gatsby she meant. By the time Jordan finished the story, we were riding in a carriage through Central Park. I said it was a strange coincidence that he lived across the bay from Daisy.

Jordan said it was not a coincidence. Gatsby had bought that house to be near her. He hoped Daisy might wander into one of his parties. When she never came, he began asking people whether they knew her. Jordan was the first person he found who did. Now he wanted me to invite Daisy to tea at my cottage and let him come over. He did not want Daisy told in advance. He wanted the meeting to seem ordinary, and he wanted her to see his house next door.

After so much preparation, the favor was so small that I found it hard to believe. I remembered Gatsby reaching out in the dark on that first night. He had been reaching toward something more particular than the stars. As the carriage went under a little bridge, I turned back to Jordan and asked her to dinner. For the first time on that ride my thoughts were of the woman beside me, not the two people she had brought together in her story.

Vocabulary List

  • lieutenant /luˈtenənt/ — A low-ranking officer in the army.
  • intently /ɪnˈtentli/ — With all your attention on one thing.
  • rumor /ˈrumər/ — A story people tell that may not be true.
  • prevented /prɪˈventɪd/ — Stopped something from happening.
  • cheerful /ˈtʃɪrfəl/ — Happy and full of good feelings.
  • devoted /dɪˈvoʊtɪd/ — Loving someone very much and giving them all your attention.
  • coincidence /koʊˈɪnsɪdəns/ — When two surprising things happen at the same time by chance.
  • wander /ˈwɑndər/ — To walk around slowly with no clear plan.
  • advance /ədˈvæns/ — Ahead of time, before something happens.
  • preparation /ˌprepəˈreɪʃən/ — The work of getting ready for something.
  • particular /pərˈtɪkjələr/ — A special or exact thing, or a certain one.
  • pearls /pɜrlz/ — Small white balls from the sea that are used to make necklaces.
