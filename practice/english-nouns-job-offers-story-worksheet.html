English Nouns — Two Job Offers

How it works

Read the story. The nouns in bold each have a blank after them. Write what kind of noun it is: proper, uncountable, possessive, compound, collective, abstract, plural, singular. Remember: a proper noun names one specific thing and starts with a capital letter; an uncountable noun has no plural; a possessive noun shows ownership (’s); a compound noun joins two words; a collective noun names a whole group; an abstract noun is an idea or a feeling; a plural noun names more than one; a singular noun names one — even if it looks plural, like news.

Examples: Ana has two job offers. The noun “Ana” is a proper noun.  ·  She needs more information. The noun “information” is uncountable.

Read the Story — Mark the Nouns (15)

✏️ Now use it: Tell your partner about two jobs — one you would like and one you would not. Use one proper noun, one collective noun, and one abstract noun — for example, “I want to work in Los Angeles” or “Happiness is more important than money.” Can your partner guess which job you would choose?
