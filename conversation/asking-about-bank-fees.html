Asking About Bank Fees

Characters:

  • MS. WILSON: A personal banker, patient and clear.
  • SAMIR: A rideshare driver confused by fees on his account.

Scene: Samir checked his bank account and saw two fees he doesn’t understand. He’s at the bank talking to Ms. Wilson.

Conversation

MS. WILSON: Good morning! How can I help you today?

SAMIR: Hi. I checked my account online, and I see two fees I don’t understand. Can you explain them?

MS. WILSON: Of course. Let me look at your account. What are the fees?

SAMIR: One is a $12 “monthly maintenance fee,” and the other is a $35 “overdraft fee.”

MS. WILSON: I see them. The $12 monthly fee applies because your balance dropped below $1,500 this month.

SAMIR: I didn’t know there was a minimum balance. How can I avoid that fee?

MS. WILSON: Three ways: keep at least $1,500 in the account, set up a monthly direct deposit of $500 or more, or switch to our basic checking account, which has no monthly fee.

SAMIR: Tell me about the basic checking account. What’s the difference?

MS. WILSON: It has no monthly fee and no minimum balance, but it doesn’t earn interest, and you get fewer free services.

SAMIR: Hmm. My pay goes in by direct deposit every two weeks, so maybe I already qualify to avoid the fee?

MS. WILSON: Let me check… Yes! You actually had qualifying deposits. The fee was charged by mistake. I’ll refund it right now.

SAMIR: Oh, that’s great news. What about the $35 overdraft fee?

MS. WILSON: That one is valid, unfortunately. Last Tuesday, a $60 payment went through when you only had $40 in the account, so we covered the difference.

SAMIR: I see. I didn’t realize my balance was that low. Can overdraft protection be turned off?

MS. WILSON: Yes — I can turn it off, so future transactions are simply declined instead of charging a fee. Many customers prefer that.

SAMIR: Please do that. I’d rather have a card declined than pay $35.

MS. WILSON: Done. I’ve also enrolled you in free balance alerts by text, so you’ll know when your balance gets low.

SAMIR: That’s really helpful. Thank you for explaining everything so clearly, Ms. Wilson.

MS. WILSON: That’s what I’m here for, Samir. Come back anytime you have questions!

— THE END —

Vocabulary List

  • Maintenance fee /ˈmeɪntənəns fiː/ (n.) a monthly charge for having an account. Example: A $12 monthly maintenance fee.
  • Overdraft fee /ˈoʊvərd ræft fiː/ (n.) a charge when you spend more than you have. Example: A $35 overdraft fee.
  • Minimum balance /ˈmɪnəməm ˈbæləns/ (n.) the lowest amount you must keep. Example: Your balance dropped below $1,500.
  • Direct deposit /dəˈrekt dɪˈpɑːzɪt/ (n.) pay sent directly to your bank. Example: Set up a monthly direct deposit.
  • Qualify /ˈkwɑːləfaɪ/ (v.) to meet the requirements. Example: You qualify to avoid the fee.
  • Refund /ˈriːfʌnd/ (v.) to give money back. Example: I’ll refund it right now.
  • Overdraft protection /ˈoʊvərd ræft prəˈtekʃən/ (n.) a service covering payments over your balance. Example: Can overdraft protection be turned off?
  • Declined /dɪˈklaɪnd/ (adj.) refused, not approved. Example: Transactions are declined instead.
  • Enroll /ɪnˈroʊl/ (v.) to sign up for a service. Example: I’ve enrolled you in balance alerts.
  • Balance alert /ˈbæləns əˈlɜːrt/ (n.) a notice about your account balance. Example: Free balance alerts by text.
