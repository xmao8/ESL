The Great Gatsby — Chapter III, Section 2: A Host No One Recognizes (Abridged Reader)

When Jordan and I went back outside, people were dancing. Some danced in pairs, while others took turns playing instruments with the orchestra. The women in yellow did a little stage act. Champagne came in glasses so large that I thought they looked like bowls. The moon shone on the water, and for a while I stopped wondering whether I belonged at the party. I was enjoying it.

Jordan and I joined a man my age and a young woman who laughed at almost everything. During a pause in the music, the man turned to me. He thought he recognized me from the war. We had served in the same division of the American army, though in different units. We talked about the wet, gray villages we remembered in France. Then he said he had bought a hydroplane, a plane that could take off from water, and invited me to go up in it the next morning. He called me “old sport,” as if we had known each other a long time.

It occurred to me that this man might know where to find our host. I told him that I lived next door, that Gatsby had sent me an invitation, and that I had not even managed to meet him. For a moment he looked puzzled. Then he told me that he was Gatsby. I apologized. I had expected an older, heavier man who would make a great show of his importance. I had not expected the quiet person who had asked me about France.

Gatsby smiled. It was the kind of smile that made you think the person in front of you understood both who you were and who you hoped to be. Then the smile was gone, and I noticed that he chose his words carefully, almost as though he were trying out an unusually formal way of speaking. A servant came up and said there was a telephone call from Chicago. Gatsby excused himself and promised to come back.

I asked Jordan what she knew about him. She said he had once told her that he went to Oxford, a university in England. She did not believe him, although she could not explain why. All evening I had heard wild explanations for his fortune. I now knew what he looked like, but I had not learned where he had come from or how he had bought the house. Jordan changed the subject. She liked large parties, she said, because you could have more privacy in them than in small ones.

The orchestra announced a new piece of jazz music. While it played, I watched Gatsby stand alone on his marble steps. He did not seem to be drinking. As his guests became louder and less careful, he seemed more formal. Some women leaned laughing against their partners, but nobody leaned against Gatsby. He looked pleased that everyone was having a good time, and remained a little apart from it, as though he were still deciding how close to come.

Before long, his servant came to ask Jordan to speak with Gatsby alone. She looked surprised but followed him into the house. It was almost two in the morning by then. Inside, I found a woman trying to sing and cry at the same time, and couples arguing about whether to go home. When Jordan finally came out of the library with Gatsby, she said he had told her something astonishing. She had promised not to repeat it. Her friends called from the porch, and before I could ask anything else, she was on her way to the door.

Adapted from F. Scott Fitzgerald, The Great Gatsby (1925), in the U.S. public domain. Read the original at gutenberg.org/ebooks/64317.

Vocabulary List

  • division /dɪˈvɪʒən/ — A large part of an army.
  • puzzled /ˈpʌzəld/ — Confused and not able to understand.
  • apologized /əˈpɑlədʒaɪzd/ — Said sorry for something.
  • formal /ˈfɔrməl/ — Serious and polite in a careful way.
  • astonishing /əˈstɑnɪʃɪŋ/ — Very surprising.
  • privacy /ˈpraɪvəsi/ — Being alone and away from other people.
  • fortune /ˈfɔrtʃən/ — A very large amount of money.
  • units /ˈjunɪts/ — Small groups that are part of a larger army.
  • remained /rɪˈmeɪnd/ — Stayed in a place or in a state.
  • partners /ˈpɑrtnɚz/ — People who dance or work together.
  • excused /ɪkˈskjust/ — Said it was all right for someone to leave.
  • recognized /ˈrɛkəɡnaɪzd/ — Knew who someone was because you had seen them before.
