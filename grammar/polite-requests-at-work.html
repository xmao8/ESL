Polite Requests at Work

At work, how you ask is as important as what you ask. A polite request gets help faster and keeps good relationships with your boss, your coworkers, and HR. This page teaches three polite ways to ask at work — and how to answer politely, yes or no.

The three forms are Would you mind…?, Could you…?, and Can you…? They all ask for the same thing, but they sound different: Would you mind is the most polite, could you is polite and friendly, and can you is friendly and common. Choose the form by the person you ask and how big the favor is.

1. “Would you mind…?” + verb-ing

Use Would you mind + verb-ing when the request is big, takes time, or goes to your boss or HR. It is the most polite form on this page.

  • Would you mind helping me with this report?
  • Would you mind closing the window? It is cold in here.
  • Would you mind explaining the new schedule to me?
  • Would you mind if I left early today?

After mind, always use the -ing form: Would you mind helping me? — not Would you mind help me?

To ask for yourself, use Would you mind if I + past simple: Would you mind if I took Friday off? / Would you mind if I used your computer? The past simple here does not mean the past — it makes the request softer.

2. “Could you…?” / “Can you…?” + base verb

Use Could you + base verb for everyday work requests. It is polite and friendly, and it works with almost everyone. Use Can you + base verb with coworkers you know well — it is friendly and very common. Could and can are modal verbs; for more about them, see Modals for Advice and Obligation.

  • Could you send me the schedule, please?
  • Could you please check this email for me?
  • Can you show me how this machine works?
  • Can you tell me when the meeting starts?

Adding please makes any request softer: Could you please help me? Put please at the end, or after could you.

Never use to after could or can: Could you send me the file? — not Could you to send me the file?

3. Formal vs Friendly vs Informal

The words are the same — the level changes with the person and the situation. Use a more polite form with your boss, HR, or a client you do not know well. Use a friendlier form with a coworker you know.

  • Formal (boss, HR, a new client): Would you mind helping me with this? / Could you please send me the file? / I was wondering if you could help me.
  • Friendly (a coworker you know): Could you help me with this? / Can you show me how it works?
  • Informal (a close coworker or friend): Can you give me a hand? / Could you do me a favor?

When you are not sure, be more polite — nobody is ever offended by a polite request. A short request with no please can sound like an order: Send me the file. sounds very different from Could you please send me the file?

4. Agreeing and Refusing Responses

A polite request needs a polite answer. When you say yes, keep it short and warm. When you say no, give a reason and offer another time — a short no alone sounds cold at work.

  • Agreeing: Sure, no problem. / Of course. / Happy to help. / No problem at all.
  • Refusing politely: I’m sorry, I can’t right now. / I’m afraid I can’t — I have a meeting. / Sorry, I’m busy now, but I can help later. / I’d love to, but I’m on a deadline.
Careful with Would you mind…? If you say Yes, it means “Yes, I mind” — that is a no. To agree, say Not at all or Of course not: Would you mind closing the door? — Not at all.

Common Mistakes

  • Using the base verb after mind: After mind, always use the -ing form.
    Incorrect: Would you mind help me? Correct: Would you mind helping me?
  • Being too direct with a boss or HR: A short order sounds rude. Add a polite form and please.
    Incorrect: Send me the file. / I want a day off. Correct: Could you please send me the file? / Would you mind if I took Friday off?
  • Answering “Yes” to Would you mind…?: Yes means you do mind — it is a no.
    Incorrect: Would you mind closing the door? — Yes. Correct: Would you mind closing the door? — Not at all. / Of course not.
  • Using can with a stranger or a client: Can you is friendly, but could you is more polite for people you do not know well.
    Too direct: Can you send me the report? (to a new client) Better: Could you please send me the report?
  • Using the present simple after Would you mind if I…: Use the past simple to make the request softer.
    Incorrect: Would you mind if I leave early? Correct: Would you mind if I left early?

Summary

  • Would you mind + verb-ing is the most polite form: Would you mind helping me? After mind, always use -ing.
  • Would you mind if I + past simple asks for yourself: Would you mind if I took Friday off?
  • Could you + base verb is polite and friendly for everyday work requests: Could you send me the schedule?
  • Can you + base verb is friendly and common with coworkers you know well: Can you show me how it works?
  • Formal (boss, HR, new client): Would you mind…? / Could you please…? Friendly (coworkers): Could you…? / Can you…? Informal (close coworkers): Can you give me a hand?
  • Agreeing: Sure, no problem. / Of course. / Happy to help. Refusing politely: I’m sorry, I can’t right now, but I can help later. Give a reason and offer another time.
  • Careful: answering Yes to Would you mind…? means no — say Not at all or Of course not to agree.

Practice by writing three polite requests: one to your boss, one to a coworker, and one to HR. Use a different form for each one.
