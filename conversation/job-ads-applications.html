Job Ads & Applications

Setting: Tuesday morning at a job fair. Ana is looking at a job ad on the board, and Luis is filling out an application at the next table. They talk about the ad, the pay, and how to apply. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Ana — works part time at a dental office. She knows a lot about jobs and likes to help her classmates find work.
  • Luis — speaks Spanish and English. He wants a full-time job with good benefits, and today he is looking for work at the job fair.

Dialogue: At the Job Fair

Ana: Luis, look at this ad! A dental office needs a receptionist.

Luis: Really? What is the pay?

Ana: It says $14–16 an hour, full time. The job offers good benefits too.

Luis: What hours? Do I need experience?

Ana: The office opens at 8 a.m. and closes at 5 p.m. Experience helps, but they train new workers.

Luis: That sounds good. How do I apply?

Ana: You can send your resume by email before Friday.

Luis: What should an applicant write on the application?

Ana: Your name, your work history, and your availability. And list two references.

Luis: I have a question. Is the job bilingual? My Spanish is very good.

Ana: Yes! The ad asks for bilingual workers. You should apply today.

Luis: I want a full-time job, and I am very dependable. Do you think I can get it?

Ana: Of course! You speak two languages, and you are reliable and detail-oriented.

Luis: What does “detail-oriented” mean on a job ad?

Ana: It means you are careful about every small thing. A receptionist must check names and numbers twice.

Luis: I can do that. I have good organizational skills too.

Ana: Great. What about the pharmacy clerk job on the next board? It pays $15 an hour.

Luis: Hmm, I prefer the receptionist job. The dental office is near my home, and I know the bus.

Ana: Good thinking. A custodian job is open at the school too, but it starts at 6 a.m.

Luis: No, thank you! I am not a morning person. I will send my resume to the dental office tonight.

Ana: Good luck! And ask the manager about the administrative assistant job too — it starts next month.

🗣️ Speaking challenge — role play (about 5 minutes)

  • Student A is the manager. Read a job ad to your partner. Tell them what the job needs: the hours, the pay, and the benefits. Use action verbs: “The office needs a receptionist. The job pays $15 an hour.”
  • Student B is the applicant. Ask three questions about the job. Use a helping verb or a modal verb in each question: “Do I need experience?” “Can I apply online?” “Must I send a resume?” “Should I bring my references?”
  • Then swap roles and try a different job: auto technician, pharmacy clerk, custodian, or administrative assistant.
  • Use at least one action verb, one state verb, and one linking verb in your talk. Use at least three of the words below.

Vocabulary List

  • Organizational skills /ɔːrɡənəˈzeɪʃənəl skɪlz/ (n.) the ability to plan your work and keep things in order. Example: Good organizational skills help a receptionist keep the office calm.
  • Applicant /ˈæplɪkənt/ (n.) a person who applies for a job. Example: Every applicant must send a resume by Friday.
  • Receptionist /rɪˈsɛpʃənɪst/ (n.) a person who answers the phone and greets people at the front desk. Example: The receptionist at the dental office is very friendly.
  • Benefits /ˈbɛnəfɪts/ (n.) good things a job gives you, like health insurance or paid vacation. Example: This job offers good benefits and a free lunch.
  • Reliable /rɪˈlaɪəbəl/ (adj.) always on time and doing what you promise; people can trust you. Example: A reliable worker never misses a day of work.
  • Pharmacy clerk /ˈfɑːrməsi klɜːrk/ (n.) a worker in a pharmacy who helps customers and sells medicine. Example: The pharmacy clerk helped me find the right medicine.
  • Experience /ɪkˈspɪəriəns/ (n.) the work you have done before. Example: The ad asks for two years of experience.
  • Detail-oriented /ˈdiːteɪl ˈɔːriɛntɪd/ (adj.) careful about every small thing. Example: A detail-oriented worker checks the numbers twice.
  • Custodian /kʌˈstoʊdiən/ (n.) a person who cleans a building, like a school or an office. Example: The custodian cleans the classrooms every night.
  • Resume /ˈrɛzəmeɪ/ (n.) a paper that lists your work history and education. Example: Send your resume to the manager by email.
  • Bilingual /baɪˈlɪŋɡwəl/ (adj.) able to speak two languages well. Example: The job ad asks for bilingual workers.
  • References /ˈrɛfərənsɪz/ (n.) people who can tell a boss you are a good worker. Example: Please list two references on your application.
  • Administrative assistant /ədˈmɪnɪstreɪtɪv əˈsɪstənt/ (n.) a person who helps in an office with letters, phone calls, and files. Example: The administrative assistant answers the phone and plans meetings.
  • Availability /əˌveɪləˈbɪləti/ (n.) the days and hours you are free to work. Example: What is your availability on weekends?
  • Dependable /dɪˈpɛndəbəl/ (adj.) always on time and doing what you promise. Example: A dependable worker never misses a day of work.
