At the Dry Cleaners

Characters:

  • MR. KIM: The owner of a neighborhood dry cleaners, experienced and detail-oriented.
  • ROSA: An office manager who needs her suit jacket cleaned by Friday.

Scene: Rosa has an important work event on Friday. She is dropping off a suit jacket with a stain at the dry cleaners. Mr. Kim is behind the counter.

Conversation

MR. KIM: Good afternoon! What can I do for you today?

ROSA: Hi! I need this suit jacket cleaned. I have an important event on Friday — is that enough time?

MR. KIM: Let me see… Today is Monday, so regular service will have it ready by Wednesday. That gives you plenty of time.

ROSA: Wonderful. There’s also a stain on the sleeve — I spilled coffee on it this morning.

MR. KIM: I see it. Coffee stains usually come out fine, but I can’t promise 100%. The sooner we treat it, the better.

ROSA: I understand. Should I point it out on the ticket, or will you remember?

MR. KIM: I’ll mark it right here on your claim ticket: “coffee stain, right sleeve.” Always check the ticket before you leave.

ROSA: Good idea. How much will it cost?

MR. KIM: A suit jacket is $8.50. Stain treatment is included in the price.

ROSA: That’s reasonable. Do I pay now or when I pick it up?

MR. KIM: You pay at pickup. We accept cash and cards.

ROSA: Perfect. What are your hours on Wednesday?

MR. KIM: We’re open 8 to 6 on weekdays, and 9 to 2 on Saturdays. We’re closed Sundays.

ROSA: I’ll come Wednesday after work, around 5:30. Will that work?

MR. KIM: Absolutely. Just bring this ticket — we need it to find your jacket.

ROSA: What happens if I lose the ticket?

MR. KIM: Don’t worry — we also tag every item with your phone number. But the ticket makes it much faster.

ROSA: I’ll keep it somewhere safe. One last question — can you press the jacket too, so it looks sharp?

MR. KIM: Pressing is always included. It’ll look like new, I promise.

ROSA: Thank you, Mr. Kim. See you Wednesday!

MR. KIM: See you then, and good luck at your event!

— THE END —

Vocabulary List

  • Dry cleaners /draɪ ˈkliːnərz/ (n.) a shop that cleans clothes with chemicals instead of water. Example: She is dropping off a jacket at the dry cleaners.
  • Stain /steɪn/ (n.) a mark on clothing that is hard to remove. Example: There’s a coffee stain on the sleeve.
  • Spill /spɪl/ (v.) to accidentally pour liquid. Example: I spilled coffee on it this morning.
  • Claim ticket /kleɪm ˈtɪkət/ (n.) a paper showing you left an item at a shop. Example: I’ll mark it on your claim ticket.
  • Treat /triːt/ (v.) to apply a cleaning process to a stain. Example: The sooner we treat it, the better.
  • Pick up /pɪk ʌp/ (phr v.) to collect something you left somewhere. Example: You pay when you pick it up.
  • Tag /tæɡ/ (v.) to attach a label with information. Example: We tag every item with your phone number.
  • Press /pres/ (v.) to iron clothes to remove wrinkles. Example: Can you press the jacket too?
  • Sharp /ʃɑːrp/ (adj.) looking neat and well-dressed. Example: So it looks sharp for the event.
  • Detail-oriented /ˈdiːteɪl ˈɔːrientɪd/ (adj.) paying close attention to small things. Example: Mr. Kim is experienced and detail-oriented.
