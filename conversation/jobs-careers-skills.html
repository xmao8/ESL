A Job Interview: Talking About Jobs & Skills

Setting: Monday morning at a medical clinic. Carlos is at a job interview for an administrative assistant job, and Ms. Rivera asks him about his skills and experience. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Carlos — a job applicant; he worked at an office for two years and wants to work at the clinic.
  • Ms. Rivera — the hiring manager at the clinic; she asks about skills, experience, and shifts.

Ms. Rivera: Good morning, Carlos. Thank you for coming in today. Please, sit down.

Carlos: Good morning, Ms. Rivera. Thank you for inviting me. I am happy to be here.

Ms. Rivera: So, you want to work as an administrative assistant at our clinic. Tell me about your experience.

Carlos: I worked at a small office for two years. I answered phones and managed schedules.

Ms. Rivera: Good. What are you good at?

Carlos: I am good at organizing information and talking to customers. I am also good at typing.

Ms. Rivera: That is important here. We are a busy clinic. Can you work under pressure?

Carlos: Yes. I enjoy working in a fast-paced environment, and I like working on a team.

Ms. Rivera: Do you speak other languages?

Carlos: Yes, I speak English and Spanish. I like helping people in both languages.

Ms. Rivera: Excellent. Are you interested in learning new skills?

Carlos: Yes, very much. I hope to learn about medical records and new computer programs.

Ms. Rivera: What shift do you prefer — morning or afternoon?

Carlos: I prefer the morning shift. I am good at starting early and working with my hands.

Ms. Rivera: One more question: why do you want this job?

Carlos: I want to help people, and I want to work at a place where I can learn and grow.

Ms. Rivera: Thank you, Carlos. We will call you this week. Do you have any questions for me?

Carlos: Yes — when does the job start, and is it full-time or part-time?

Ms. Rivera: It starts next month, and it is full-time. We hope to hire someone soon.

Carlos: Thank you very much, Ms. Rivera. I look forward to hearing from you.

🎯 Speaking Challenge

  • Use 3 new vocabulary words in your conversation.
  • Answer as YOURSELF: what job would you like, and what are you good at? Use want to, enjoy + -ing, and good at + -ing.
  • Switch roles: one person is the interviewer, the other is the applicant. Talk about YOUR life, not just the characters'.

Vocabulary List

  • Resume /rɪˈzuːmeɪ/ (n.) a short written list of your jobs, skills, and education. Example: Bring your resume to the interview tomorrow.
  • Interview /ˈɪntərvjuː/ (n.) a meeting where an employer asks you questions about a job. Example: I have a job interview on Monday morning.
  • Skill /skɪl/ (n.) something you can do well, learned from study or practice. Example: Talking to customers is an important skill for a server.
  • Experience /ɪkˈspɪəriəns/ (n.) the jobs and work you have done before. Example: She has five years of experience as a dental hygienist.
  • Apply /əˈplaɪ/ (v.) to ask for a job by sending your resume or filling out a form. Example: I want to apply for the job at the clinic.
  • Hire /ˈhaɪər/ (v.) to give someone a job. Example: The clinic hopes to hire a home health aide this month.
  • Salary /ˈsæləri/ (n.) the money you earn for a job, usually paid every month. Example: The salary for this job is good, and the hours are fair.
  • Shift /ʃɪft/ (n.) a set time to work, such as morning or night. Example: I work the morning shift, from 8 to 4.
  • Part-time /ˈpɑːrt taɪm/ (adj.) working only some hours of the week, not full-time. Example: She has a part-time job at a dental office.
  • Coworker /ˈkoʊwɜːrkər/ (n.) a person you work with at the same job. Example: My coworkers help me when I have a question.
