Online or In-Store?

Setting: Sunday evening at Luis's apartment. Maya and Luis need a birthday gift for their friend Rosa, whose birthday is on Friday. They can't agree on whether to shop online or go to the mall. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Maya — loves the convenience of online shopping; always hunting for discount codes.
  • Luis — prefers real stores; likes to see and touch things before buying.

Maya: Rosa's birthday is Friday, and we still don't have a gift!

Luis: I know. So — should we order something online or go to the mall?

Maya: Online is so convenient. I can order from my couch right now.

Luis: But shipping takes three days. What if it arrives late?

Maya: We can pay for fast delivery. It's only a few dollars more.

Luis: Those “few dollars” add up. And if she doesn't like it, returns are such a pain.

Maya: Most websites have free returns now. You just print a label and drop off the box.

Luis: Still, you can't try things on or see the real color online. Remember that shirt I ordered? It looked blue on screen, but it was green!

Maya: (Laughs) That was pretty bad. Okay, what would you get her in a store?

Luis: Maybe a nice scarf or a candle set. Good quality you can feel with your hands.

Maya: A scarf is risky — what if it's the wrong style? At least online I can read the reviews first.

Luis: Reviews help, but in a store I can touch the fabric and check the quality myself.

Maya: True. And honestly, shopping together is more fun. We never do that anymore.

Luis: Exactly! We can grab lunch at the mall too. Shopping online is kind of lonely.

Maya: But the mall is so crowded, and parking is terrible.

Luis: We could go on a weekday evening. It'll be much quieter.

Maya: Hmm, wait — Rosa sent me her wishlist link last week. Let me check it.

Luis: Oh! If she already picked something out, ordering online makes total sense. No guessing.

Maya: It's a set of watercolor paints. And I have a discount code for fifteen percent off!

Luis: Even better. Let's order it now and choose fast shipping so it arrives by Thursday.

Maya: Done. I'll use the code at checkout. Total is thirty-four dollars with shipping.

Luis: Not bad. And if the colors are wrong, we can always exchange it.

Maya: See? Online shopping wins this time.

Luis: This time. But the next gift, we're going to the mall together.

Maya: (Smiling) Agreed. Now help me write the card — you're better with words.

Luis: “Happy birthday, Rosa! May your year be as colorful as these paints!”

🎯 Speaking Challenge

  • Use 3 new vocabulary words in your conversation.
  • Talk about YOUR life, not just the characters'.
  • After you finish: your teacher will share useful corrections they heard — during the conversation, focus on speaking, not on being perfect.

Vocabulary List

  • Shipping /ˈʃɪpɪŋ/ (n.) the cost or process of sending a product to your home. Example: Shipping is free on orders over fifty dollars.
  • Return /rɪˈtɜːrn/ (v.) to bring a product back to the store. Example: I need to return these shoes — they're too small.
  • Refund /ˈriːfʌnd/ (n.) money given back to you when you return a product. Example: The store gave me a full refund.
  • Try on /traɪ ɑːn/ (phr v.) to put on clothing to see if it fits. Example: Can I try on this jacket?
  • Review /rɪˈvjuː/ (n.) an opinion about a product written by a buyer. Example: I read the reviews before I ordered.
  • Discount code /ˈdɪskaʊnt koʊd/ (n.) letters or numbers you enter online to pay less. Example: Use this discount code for fifteen percent off.
  • Cart /kɑːrt/ (n.) the online list of items you plan to buy. Example: I have three things in my cart.
  • Delivery /dɪˈlɪvəri/ (n.) bringing a product to your home or office. Example: Delivery takes two to three days.
  • In stock /ɪn stɑːk/ (phrase) available to buy right now. Example: The blue one is out of stock, but red is in stock.
  • Exchange /ɪksˈtʃeɪndʒ/ (v.) to replace a product with a different one. Example: Can I exchange this for a larger size?
