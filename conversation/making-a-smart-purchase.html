Making a Smart Purchase

Setting: A weekday evening at Sofia's kitchen table. Sofia's old car keeps breaking down, and she wants to buy a used car. Her friend David is helping her make a careful plan before she spends her money. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Sofia — needs a used car; has a strict budget and has never negotiated a price before.
  • David — Sofia's practical friend; believes smart shoppers always plan before they pay.

Sofia: My car is falling apart. I think it's finally time to buy a used one.

David: That's a big purchase. Let's make a smart plan first, before you look at any cars.

Sofia: Good idea. My budget is twelve thousand dollars. Not a dollar more.

David: Okay, that's our limit. Now — what are your must-haves?

Sofia: It must be reliable, good on gas, and have room for the kids' car seats.

David: And your nice-to-haves?

Sofia: A backup camera would be nice. And Bluetooth. But I can live without them.

David: Smart. Knowing the difference between must-have and nice-to-have saves a lot of money.

Sofia: I found three cars online. This one is ten thousand five hundred, with low mileage.

David: Low mileage is good, but let's check the full history. Has it been in any accidents?

Sofia: The ad says “clean title.” What does that mean?

David: It means no serious accidents or damage were reported. But we should verify it ourselves.

Sofia: How do we do that?

David: We can look up the car's history online with its VIN number. And we must inspect the car in person.

Sofia: Can we take it to a mechanic before buying?

David: Yes! A pre-purchase inspection costs about a hundred dollars, but it can save you thousands.

Sofia: What about negotiating the price? I've never done that. It feels rude.

David: It's not rude — it's normal. Sellers expect it. If they ask eleven thousand, you can offer ten.

Sofia: And if they say no?

David: Then you walk away. There are always more cars. Never let anyone pressure you.

Sofia: What about a loan? I can't pay the full price in cash.

David: You'd make a small down payment, then pay the rest in monthly installments. But compare interest rates at two banks first.

Sofia: This is a lot to remember. Will you come with me on Saturday?

David: Of course. We'll test-drive it, inspect it, and negotiate together.

Sofia: Thank you. With a real plan, I feel much less nervous.

David: That's the secret — smart shoppers plan before they pay.

🎯 Speaking Challenge

  • Use 3 new vocabulary words in your conversation.
  • Talk about YOUR life, not just the characters'.
  • After you finish: your teacher will share useful corrections they heard — during the conversation, focus on speaking, not on being perfect.

Vocabulary List

  • Budget /ˈbʌdʒɪt/ (n.) the amount of money you plan to spend. Example: My budget for the car is twelve thousand dollars.
  • Negotiate /nɪˈɡoʊʃieɪt/ (v.) to discuss a price until both sides agree. Example: She negotiated the price down by one thousand dollars.
  • Down payment /daʊn ˈpeɪmənt/ (n.) money paid first when buying something expensive. Example: We made a two-thousand-dollar down payment on the car.
  • Mileage /ˈmaɪlɪdʒ/ (n.) the number of miles a car has been driven. Example: Low mileage usually means less wear on the car.
  • Must-have /ˈmʌst hæv/ (n. phrase) something you really need; not optional. Example: Air conditioning is a must-have in the summer.
  • Trade-in /ˈtreɪd ɪn/ (n.) your old car given to the dealer to lower the new price. Example: They gave me three thousand dollars for my trade-in.
  • Installment /ɪnˈstɔːlmənt/ (n.) one of several payments made over time. Example: We pay the loan in monthly installments.
  • Loan /loʊn/ (n.) money you borrow and pay back later, usually with interest. Example: The bank approved my car loan.
  • Inspect /ɪnˈspɛkt/ (v.) to look at something very carefully. Example: Always inspect a used car before you buy it.
  • Deal-breaker /ˈdiːl ˌbreɪkər/ (n.) a problem so serious that you will not buy. Example: A bad accident history is a deal-breaker for me.
