Renewing a Lease

Characters:

  • MR. GARCIA: The landlord, businesslike but fair.
  • HANNAH: A tenant who wants to renew her lease at a fair price.

Scene: Hannah’s one-year lease ends next month. She likes the apartment but the rent is going up. She’s meeting Mr. Garcia, her landlord, to discuss renewal.

Conversation

MR. GARCIA: Hi, Hannah. Thanks for meeting with me. Your lease ends on October 31st — have you thought about renewing?

HANNAH: I have, and I’d love to stay. I really like the apartment and the neighborhood.

MR. GARCIA: I’m glad to hear that — you’ve been a great tenant. I’m offering a 12-month renewal at $1,650 a month.

HANNAH: I see. That’s $100 more than I’m paying now. That’s a pretty big increase.

MR. GARCIA: I understand it feels like a lot. But property taxes and insurance went up this year, so I had to adjust.

HANNAH: I understand costs go up. Would you consider meeting in the middle at $1,600? I’ve always paid on time and never caused any problems.

MR. GARCIA: You have been an excellent tenant, that’s true. Let me think… I could do $1,620. That’s the best I can offer.

HANNAH: I appreciate that. $1,620 works for me. Would the other terms stay the same?

MR. GARCIA: Yes — same terms, same security deposit. The new lease would run November 1st through October 31st.

HANNAH: One question: the dishwasher has been leaking a little. Could that be fixed before I sign?

MR. GARCIA: Absolutely. I’ll send my handyman this week. I want everything working properly.

HANNAH: Thank you. When do you need my decision?

MR. GARCIA: If you could sign by the 15th, that would be great. That gives us both enough time.

HANNAH: I’ll sign this week. Can you email me the new lease so I can read it first?

MR. GARCIA: Of course. I’ll send it tonight. Everything will be the same except the rent amount and the dates.

HANNAH: Perfect. I’m happy we could work this out, Mr. Garcia.

MR. GARCIA: Me too, Hannah. Good tenants are hard to find — I’m glad you’re staying.

— THE END —

Vocabulary List

  • Lease /liːs/ (n.) a legal contract to rent a place. Example: Your lease ends October 31st.
  • Renew /rɪˈnuː/ (v.) to extend a contract for another period. Example: Have you thought about renewing?
  • Tenant /ˈtenənt/ (n.) a person who rents a home. Example: You’ve been a great tenant.
  • Landlord /ˈlændlɔːrd/ (n.) the owner of a rented property. Example: She’s meeting her landlord.
  • Increase /ɪnˈkriːs/ (n.) a rise in amount. Example: That’s a pretty big increase.
  • Meet in the middle /miːt ɪn ðə ˈmɪdəl/ (phr.) to compromise. Example: Would you meet in the middle at $1,600?
  • Security deposit /səˈkjʊrəti dɪˈpɑːzɪt/ (n.) money held by the landlord as protection. Example: Same terms, same security deposit.
  • Terms /tɜːrmz/ (n.) the conditions of an agreement. Example: Would the other terms stay the same?
  • Handyman /ˈhændimæn/ (n.) a person who does small repairs. Example: I’ll send my handyman this week.
  • Work out /wɜːrk aʊt/ (phr v.) to resolve successfully. Example: I’m happy we could work this out.
