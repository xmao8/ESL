Talking About Your Hometown

Setting: Tuesday evening in the campus cafeteria. Aisha and Marco are new coworkers eating lunch together, and they discover they both grew up in small towns — but very different ones. They compare their hometowns: jobs, schools, neighborhoods, and bank branches. (About 5 minutes of pair speaking practice.)

Characters:

  • Aisha — grew up in Millbrook, a quiet town of 8,000 people; she misses it but loves city life now.
  • Marco — grew up in Riverside, a busy city of 300,000 people; his whole family still lives there.

Aisha: So, Marco — where did you grow up? I'm trying to picture you as a kid.

Marco: Riverside, about two hours south of here. And you?

Aisha: Millbrook. It's tiny — only about 8,000 people. Riverside has way more people than my hometown.

Marco: That's true! Riverside has more shopping malls than I can count. What about Millbrook — what is it known for?

Aisha: Farms and quiet streets. My town has less traffic noise than Riverside, that's for sure. What did you like about growing up there?

Marco: The energy. Riverside has more job opportunities than any place nearby, so my older brother never had to leave to find work.

Aisha: Lucky him. Millbrook has fewer job opportunities than Riverside — most of my friends moved away after high school. That's actually why I'm here.

Marco: Makes sense. Does your family still live in Millbrook?

Aisha: Yes. My parents love it — they say Millbrook has more friendly people than the city, and I think they're right.

Marco: That's sweet. Riverside has friendly people too, but honestly, I don't even know my neighbors' names.

Aisha: See? That's the small-town thing. Millbrook also has the most affordable housing of any town in the county. My parents bought their house for less than the cost of a garage here.

Marco: Wow. Riverside definitely doesn't have that. But we have better public schools — Riverside has more good schools than Millbrook.

Aisha: True, Millbrook has fewer schools, but mine was great. Small classes. Our teachers knew every student.

Marco: What about safety? My mom worries about crime a lot.

Aisha: Millbrook has the least crime of any town I know. We never even locked our front door.

Marco: I can't imagine that. Riverside has more crime than I'd like, but my neighborhood is pretty safe.

Aisha: Speaking of work — your brother, the one who stayed. What does he do?

Marco: He's a teller at a bank downtown. The downtown branch has fewer tellers than the main office, so his lines are always long.

Aisha: My cousin works at a bank too! She's a loan officer. She needs more training than a customer service representative, so she's taking classes at night.

Marco: That's hard work. And your town — does Millbrook even have a bank? It's so small.

Aisha: Just one! Millbrook has the fewest banks of any town in the county — one tiny branch. Everyone knows the manager by name.

Marco: (Laughs) Riverside has more banks than restaurants, I swear. Honestly, I think I'd like to visit Millbrook someday. It sounds peaceful.

Aisha: You should come! But be warned — Millbrook has less nightlife than a library. We go to bed early.

Marco: Deal. And you should visit Riverside — I'll show you that it has more good restaurants than you can visit in one weekend.

🎯 Speaking Challenge

  • Use 3 new vocabulary words in your conversation.
  • Talk about YOUR life, not just the characters'.
  • After you finish: your teacher will share useful corrections they heard — during the conversation, focus on speaking, not on being perfect.

Vocabulary List

  • Hometown /ˈhoʊmˌtaʊn/ (n.) the town or city where you were born or grew up. Example: She returns to her hometown every summer to visit family.
  • Neighborhood /ˈneɪbərˌhʊd/ (n.) a small area inside a town or city, often where people live. Example: Our neighborhood is quiet and full of friendly people.
  • Affordable housing /əˈfɔrdəbəl ˈhaʊzɪŋ/ (n. phrase) homes that people can buy or rent without paying too much. Example: This town is famous for its affordable housing.
  • Job opportunity /dʒɑb ˌɑpərˈtunəti/ (n. phrase) a chance to get a job. Example: The new factory brings many job opportunities to our town.
  • Public schools /ˈpʌblɪk skulz/ (n. pl.) schools that are free and paid for by the government. Example: Parents move here because of the good public schools.
  • Bank branch /bæŋk bræntʃ/ (n.) one office of a bank in a town or city. Example: The bank branch on Main Street closes at five.
  • Teller /ˈtɛlər/ (n.) a bank worker who takes and gives money to customers. Example: The teller counted my cash and gave me a receipt.
  • Loan officer /loʊn ˈɔfɪsər/ (n.) a bank worker who decides if people can borrow money. Example: The loan officer approved our application for a car loan.
  • Community /kəˈmjunəti/ (n.) the people who live in one place, or the feeling of belonging to them. Example: The festival brings the whole community together.
  • Commute /kəˈmjut/ (n./v.) the trip between home and work; to make that trip. Example: Her commute is only fifteen minutes by bus.
