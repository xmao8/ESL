Renting a Car

Characters:

  • RACHEL: A rental agent, professional and thorough.
  • PETER: A teacher renting a car for the weekend.

Scene: Peter’s car is in the shop, and he needs to visit his parents this weekend. He’s at a car rental office talking to Rachel.

Conversation

RACHEL: Hi! Welcome to City Rentals. Are you picking up a reservation, or would you like to rent today?

PETER: I’d like to rent a car for the weekend. My car is in the shop.

RACHEL: I can help with that. What size car do you need — economy, midsize, or SUV?

PETER: Just an economy car. It’s only me, and I’m driving about 200 miles round trip.

RACHEL: An economy car is perfect then — and great on gas. When do you need it?

PETER: I’d like to pick it up Friday afternoon and return it Monday morning.

RACHEL: Let me check availability… Good news — we have economy cars available. The weekend rate is $45 per day.

PETER: Does that include insurance, or is that extra?

RACHEL: Basic insurance is included. We also offer full coverage for an extra $18 per day — it covers any damage with no deductible.

PETER: Hmm. My own car insurance might already cover rentals. Can I check with my insurance company first?

RACHEL: Absolutely — many policies do cover rentals. Just call them before Friday. If not, you can add our coverage at pickup.

PETER: I will. What do I need to bring when I pick up the car?

RACHEL: Your driver’s license and a credit card in your name. We place a $200 hold on the card as a deposit.

PETER: A hold — so it’s not actually charged?

RACHEL: Correct. It’s just a temporary authorization. It’s released when you return the car in good condition.

PETER: What’s the fuel policy? Do I return it full?

RACHEL: Yes — “full to full.” You’ll receive it with a full tank, so please bring it back full to avoid refueling charges.

PETER: Understood. Is there a mileage limit?

RACHEL: You get 150 miles per day included. After that, it’s 25 cents per extra mile.

PETER: 200 miles over three days is well within the limit. Let’s book it!

RACHEL: Wonderful. I’ll need your license and card to complete the reservation.

PETER: Here you go. Thanks for explaining everything, Rachel.

RACHEL: My pleasure! See you Friday — have a great trip!

— THE END —

Vocabulary List

  • Reservation /ˌrezərˈveɪʃən/ (n.) a booking made in advance. Example: Are you picking up a reservation?
  • Economy car /ɪˈkɑːnəmi kɑːr/ (n.) a small, affordable rental car. Example: Just an economy car.
  • Round trip /raʊnd trɪp/ (n.) going somewhere and coming back. Example: 200 miles round trip.
  • Deductible /dɪˈdʌktəbəl/ (n.) money you pay before insurance covers. Example: No deductible with full coverage.
  • Hold /hoʊld/ (n.) a temporary charge on a card. Example: We place a $200 hold as a deposit.
  • Released /rɪˈliːst/ (adj.) freed; no longer held. Example: It’s released when you return the car.
  • Fuel policy /ˈfjuːəl ˈpɑːləsi/ (n.) rules about gasoline when renting. Example: What’s the fuel policy?
  • Full to full /fʊl tə fʊl/ (phr.) return the car with a full tank. Example: “Full to full” — bring it back full.
  • Refueling charge /riːˈfjuːəlɪŋ tʃɑːrdʒ/ (n.) a fee if you don’t refill the tank. Example: To avoid refueling charges.
  • Mileage limit /ˈmaɪlɪdʒ ˈlɪmɪt/ (n.) maximum miles included. Example: 150 miles per day included.
