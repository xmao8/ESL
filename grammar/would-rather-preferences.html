Would Rather — Saying What You Prefer

Would rather means “prefer.” Use it to say what you choose when you have two options. It is common in job interviews, when you talk about work, hours, and the kind of job you want.

1. Would rather + base verb

Form: subject + would rather + base verb (no “to” after rather).

  • I would rather work outdoors than in an office.
  • She would rather wear a long-sleeved shirt than a t-shirt to the interview.
  • They would rather work on a team than work alone.
  • He would rather be a general manager than a teacher.

Use than to show the second choice: I would rather arrive early than be late.

Short form: I’d rather work outdoors. In speaking, “would” is often shortened to ’d.

2. Would rather not — saying no politely

Put not after rather: would rather not + base verb.

  • I would rather not work on weekends.
  • She would rather not talk about her salary in the first interview.
  • He would rather not wear a suit every day.

This is a polite, soft way to say no: I would rather not work nights sounds kinder than I don’t want to work nights.

3. Questions: Would you rather … or …?

Form: Would + subject + rather + base verb … or …? Keep both choices parallel (the same form).

  • Would you rather work in an office or outdoors?
  • Would you rather have high pay or good hours?
  • Would you rather be a general manager or a teacher?

Answer with the full form: I would rather work outdoors, because I like fresh air.

4. Would rather + subject + past — a polite wish about someone else

When you talk about what you want someone else to do, use would rather + subject + past simple. The past form talks about the present or future, not the past.

  • I would rather you wore a suit to the interview. (= Please wear a suit.)
  • She would rather he arrived ten minutes early.
  • We would rather the manager told us about the team first.
Use the past form even for now: wore, not wear; arrived, not arrive.

Common Mistakes

  • Using “to” after would rather: Remember, never use “to” after rather.
    Incorrect: I would rather to work outdoors. Correct: I would rather work outdoors.
  • Using the present form after would rather + subject: Use the past form for the other person’s action.
    Incorrect: I would rather you wear a suit. Correct: I would rather you wore a suit.
  • Forgetting “or” in a question: A “would you rather” question needs two choices joined by or.
    Incorrect: Would you rather work in an office? Correct: Would you rather work in an office or outdoors?

Summary

  • Would rather + base verb says what you prefer: I would rather work on a team.
  • Would rather not + base verb is a polite no: I would rather not work nights.
  • Would you rather … or …? asks someone to choose between two options.
  • Would rather + subject + past simple is a polite wish about someone else: I would rather you wore a suit.

Practice by making your own “would rather” sentences about jobs — what kind of work, hours, and team do you prefer?
