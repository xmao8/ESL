Following Up After a Job Interview

Characters:

  • MS. THOMPSON: The hiring manager at a logistics company, professional and direct.
  • JAVIER: An applicant following up after his interview.

Scene: Javier interviewed for a delivery driver position three days ago and hasn’t heard back. He’s calling Ms. Thompson, the hiring manager, to follow up.

Conversation

Javier takes a deep breath and dials the number from the business card.

MS. THOMPSON: Hello, this is Angela Thompson.

JAVIER: Hello, Ms. Thompson. This is Javier Morales. I interviewed for the delivery driver position on Tuesday.

MS. THOMPSON: Hello, Javier! Yes, I remember. Thanks for calling.

JAVIER: I wanted to follow up and say thank you for meeting with me. I’m very interested in the position.

MS. THOMPSON: I appreciate that. It’s always good to hear from candidates after the interview.

JAVIER: I also wanted to ask about the timeline — when do you expect to make a decision?

MS. THOMPSON: We’re finishing interviews this week. We should have a decision by next Wednesday.

JAVIER: That’s helpful to know. Is there any additional information I can provide in the meantime?

MS. THOMPSON: Actually, yes. Could you send me a copy of your driving record? We require it for all driver positions.

JAVIER: Of course. I can get that from the DMV website today and email it to you. What’s the best email address?

MS. THOMPSON: Send it to t.thompson@fastfreight.com. And Javier — your interview went very well.

JAVIER: Thank you, that’s encouraging to hear. I have five years of delivery experience and a clean driving record.

MS. THOMPSON: That’s exactly what stood out to us. Just get me that driving record, and you’ll be all set.

JAVIER: I’ll send it within the hour. One more question — if I’m selected, when would the start date be?

MS. THOMPSON: We’d like someone to start on the first of next month, after a week of paid training.

JAVIER: That works perfectly for me. Thank you again for your time, Ms. Thompson.

MS. THOMPSON: You’re welcome, Javier. And good luck — we’ll be in touch soon.

JAVIER: Have a great day!

Javier hangs up, smiling, and immediately opens the DMV website on his phone.

— THE END —

Vocabulary List

  • Follow up /ˈfɑːloʊ ʌp/ (phr v.) to contact someone again about something. Example: I wanted to follow up after my interview.
  • Hiring manager /ˈhaɪərɪŋ ˈmænɪdʒər/ (n.) the person who decides who gets hired. Example: She’s calling the hiring manager.
  • Candidate /ˈkændədeɪt/ (n.) a person being considered for a job. Example: It’s good to hear from candidates.
  • Timeline /ˈtaɪmlaɪn/ (n.) the schedule for when things will happen. Example: When do you expect to make a decision?
  • Driving record /ˈdraɪvɪŋ ˈrekərd/ (n.) an official history of your driving. Example: Send me a copy of your driving record.
  • In the meantime /ɪn ðə ˈmiːntaɪm/ (phr.) during the time while waiting. Example: Anything I can provide in the meantime?
  • Stand out /stænd aʊt/ (phr v.) to be noticeable in a good way. Example: That’s what stood out to us.
  • Clean driving record /kliːn ˈdraɪvɪŋ ˈrekərd/ (phr.) no accidents or tickets. Example: I have a clean driving record.
  • Paid training /peɪd ˈtreɪnɪŋ/ (n.) job training you receive wages for. Example: After a week of paid training.
  • Be in touch /biː ɪn tʌtʃ/ (phr.) to contact someone later. Example: We’ll be in touch soon.
