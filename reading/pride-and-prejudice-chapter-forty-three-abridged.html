Pride and Prejudice — Chapter Forty-Three (Abridged)

As their carriage approached Pemberley, Elizabeth felt nervous excitement. The park was huge and beautiful, with woods, hills, and a natural stream. When the house first came into view, she was amazed — it was large and elegant, but not overdone or artificial. She thought to herself that being the mistress of such a place would truly mean something.

As they got closer to the house, Elizabeth's fear returned — what if the housekeeper had been wrong, and Mr. Darcy was actually home? Inside, a kind, respectable older housekeeper named Mrs. Reynolds greeted them and led them through beautifully decorated rooms with lovely views. Elizabeth couldn't help thinking, "I might have been mistress of this place." But she quickly reminded herself that marrying Darcy would have meant losing her close relationship with her aunt and uncle, since he never would have allowed her to invite them.

Elizabeth wanted to ask whether Mr. Darcy was truly away, but felt too nervous. Luckily, her uncle asked instead. Mrs. Reynolds confirmed that he was gone, though he was expected back the next day with several guests. Elizabeth felt great relief that their visit hadn't been delayed by even one day.

While admiring the paintings, Elizabeth's aunt pointed out a small portrait of Mr. Wickham. The housekeeper mentioned that he was the son of the former steward, raised by the family, but had since become "very wild." Elizabeth said nothing.

Then, unexpectedly, Mrs. Reynolds began describing her master in the warmest way possible — calling him the kindest, most generous person she had ever known, someone who had never once spoken a harsh word to her, even as a small child. Elizabeth listened in complete amazement. This description was nothing like the proud, unpleasant man she had always imagined.

Later, walking through a gallery of family portraits, Elizabeth stopped in front of a large painting of Mr. Darcy himself. The kind expression on his painted face reminded her of moments when he had looked at her that way in real life. Standing there, she felt warmer, gentler feelings toward him than she ever had before.

As they left the house to walk through the gardens, Mr. Darcy suddenly appeared, walking toward them from the direction of the stables. Both Elizabeth and Darcy blushed deeply, clearly shocked to see each other. He recovered quickly and spoke politely, asking about her family and her stay in Derbyshire, though his voice sounded nervous and rushed. After a few awkward minutes, he excused himself and left.

Elizabeth felt embarrassed, worried that Darcy might think she had planned this meeting on purpose. As they continued walking, Darcy reappeared. This time, Elizabeth stayed calm and greeted him properly. To her surprise, he asked to be introduced to her aunt and uncle — the very same "unsuitable" relatives he had once looked down on. He treated them with genuine warmth, even inviting Mr. Gardiner to go fishing on his land anytime he liked.

As they walked together, Darcy mentioned that Mr. Bingley and his sisters would soon arrive at Pemberley — and that his own sister, Georgiana, was eager to meet Elizabeth as well. Elizabeth was stunned but pleased. Clearly, Darcy no longer resented her refusal.

Afterward, her aunt and uncle couldn't stop praising Darcy's excellent manners, completely surprised that he seemed nothing like the proud man they had heard about. Elizabeth quietly tried to explain that Darcy's true character was very different from what people believed — and that Wickham's reputation deserved far less trust than everyone assumed.

Vocabulary List

  • nervous — feeling anxious or worried.
  • elegant — graceful and stylish in appearance.
  • artificial — made or created by humans, not natural.
  • relief — a feeling of comfort after worry or stress has ended.
  • steward — a person who manages another person's property or affairs.
  • generous — willing to give freely; kind and giving.
  • amazement — a feeling of great surprise or wonder.
  • portrait — a painting or drawing of a person.
  • blush — to become red in the face from embarrassment.
  • awkward — causing embarrassment or discomfort.
  • embarrassed — feeling self-conscious or ashamed in front of others.
  • genuine — real and sincere, not fake.
  • resent — to feel bitter or angry about something unfair.
  • reputation — the general opinion people have about someone's character.
  • stunned — extremely surprised or shocked.
