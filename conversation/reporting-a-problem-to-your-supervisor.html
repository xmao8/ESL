Reporting a Problem to Your Supervisor

Characters:

  • LISA: A supervisor at a food processing plant, safety-conscious and calm.
  • MOHAMED: A machine operator who spotted a problem early.

Scene: Mohamed noticed that one of the machines on the factory floor is making a strange noise. He reports it to Lisa, his supervisor, before it becomes dangerous.

Conversation

MOHAMED: Lisa, do you have a moment? I need to report something on line three.

LISA: Of course, Mohamed. What’s going on?

MOHAMED: Machine number five is making a loud grinding noise. It started about an hour ago, and it’s getting worse.

LISA: A grinding noise? Did you stop the machine?

MOHAMED: Yes, I shut it down right away and put up the warning sign, like we learned in training.

LISA: Good — that was exactly the right thing to do. Did you notice anything else unusual?

MOHAMED: There was a burning smell too, kind of like hot metal. That’s when I decided to stop it.

LISA: I’m glad you trusted your instincts. A burning smell can mean the motor is overheating.

MOHAMED: Should I have called maintenance directly instead of coming to you?

LISA: No, you did it correctly. Always tell your supervisor first, and I’ll contact maintenance. That way everything is documented.

MOHAMED: Okay. How long do you think the machine will be down?

LISA: Hard to say until maintenance looks at it. I’ll move you to line two for now so you can keep working.

MOHAMED: That works for me. Will this affect our production target for today?

LISA: It might, but safety comes before numbers. I’d rather miss a target than have someone get hurt.

MOHAMED: I agree completely. Is there an incident report I should fill out?

LISA: Yes, I’ll send you the form. Just describe what you heard and smelled, and what time it started.

MOHAMED: I’ll do it right after my break. Thanks for taking this seriously, Lisa.

LISA: Thank you for speaking up, Mohamed. Reporting problems early is how we keep everyone safe.

MOHAMED: I’ll keep an eye — and an ear — on the other machines too.

LISA: That’s the spirit. Let me go call maintenance now.

— THE END —

Vocabulary List

  • Report /rɪˈpɔːrt/ (v.) to tell someone in charge about something. Example: I need to report something.
  • Grinding noise /ˈɡraɪndɪŋ nɔɪz/ (n.) a harsh scraping sound. Example: The machine is making a grinding noise.
  • Shut down /ʃʌt daʊn/ (phr v.) to turn off a machine. Example: I shut it down right away.
  • Warning sign /ˈwɔːrnɪŋ saɪn/ (n.) a sign alerting people to danger. Example: I put up the warning sign.
  • Trust your instincts /trʌst jɔːr ˈɪnstɪŋkts/ (phr.) to act on your gut feeling. Example: I’m glad you trusted your instincts.
  • Overheat /ˌoʊvərˈhiːt/ (v.) to become too hot. Example: The motor is overheating.
  • Documented /ˈdɑːkjəmentɪd/ (adj.) officially recorded in writing. Example: I’ll contact maintenance so it’s documented.
  • Production target /prəˈdʌkʃən ˈtɑːrɡɪt/ (n.) the amount a factory must produce. Example: Will this affect our production target?
  • Incident report /ˈɪnsədənt rɪˈpɔːrt/ (n.) a written record of a problem. Example: Fill out an incident report.
  • Speak up /spiːk ʌp/ (phr v.) to say something, especially a concern. Example: Thank you for speaking up.
